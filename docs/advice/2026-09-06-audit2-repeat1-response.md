## Binding interpretation of “cheap d0/d1”

The diagnostic cutpoints are allowed only because they are already-prepared
three-action and nineteen-action fresh-process probes that may reveal a shared
base-loader defect.  They are not a new milestone and do not get an open-ended
debugging budget.

The operating rule is now:

1. d0 and d1 each receive a hard one-hour wall/CPU containment limit.
2. d1 runs only after d0 has executed and passed its independent consumer.
3. A timeout, functional failure, consumer rejection, or need for a
   bootstrap-scale repair is preserved as diagnostic evidence; it does not
   start a repair loop before Great 100.
4. Subject to the already-passed parser and clean-authority gates, the complete
   schema-7 Great-100 diagnostic then runs even if d0 or d1 did not pass.
5. No compatibility change is developed until the available parser, direct,
   and Great-100 failure sets have been collected and classified together.

This distinguishes the hard process limit from the smaller human diagnosis
budget recommended by the audit.  The process may take up to one hour, but it
gets no subsequent 30–60-minute investigation that delays Great 100.

The previously queued d0/d1 and Great-100 waiter processes used the older
86,400-second direct containment and required an exact d1 pass before Great
100.  They were stopped while still sleeping, before any direct result,
transition record, diagnostic build, or Great-100 destination existed.  The
replacement handoff uses the rule above.  No running compiler, parser, Candle
child, or PFT process was signalled.

## Side-work rule

The repeat audit's proposed waiting-time rule is adopted verbatim in substance.
Until diagnostic Great 100 is 65/65, idle capacity may be used only for work
that can plausibly shorten or improve the next functional failure batch.

Allowed examples are:

- auditing or preparing the exact parser/direct/Great-100 launch commands;
- parser/localizer tooling for an observed or imminent corpus failure;
- focused OCaml differential tests for syntax or runtime constructs in the
  selected source closure;
- static reachability checks for APIs used by Great 100 or Flyspeck; and
- minimization and batching of actual compatibility failures.

Disallowed examples are:

- checkpoint or namespace architecture;
- hostile-host hardening;
- new receipt or provenance schemas;
- generalized cache or release architecture;
- x64-only specialization or proof-translation optimization; and
- speculative large-theory splitting.

The two read-only handoff audits performed during the current bootstrap satisfy
this rule: one checked the exact parser 20/400 producer-consumer contract, and
one checked the already-prepared schema-7 Great-100 transition and launch.
They found no implementation blocker and directly reduced the risk of wasting
the just-built runtime on a stale command or destination.

## Serializer-authority decision exposed by the repair

approved theorem, hypothesis, conclusion, and global-axiom hashes for both
`ARITHMETIC_GEOMETRIC_MEAN` targets.  This is strong compatibility evidence,
but it is deliberately **not** S1 evidence.

The approved authority pins serializer SHA-256
`ec150fcb1af48c5554cf535b0b076b0607e44e67d12367b7a0fb8a565e5639ff`.
The repaired serializer is
`c9fa348046166cac736c325ac2e840134e07de6493d44c9d75d26935aef7da48`.

mismatch, as it should.  Replacing the pinned hash in place would falsely

comments that load out.

closure, so `candle/compute.ml` has been removed from the default base in its

and 3 axioms, versus 24, 360, 316, and 3.  Thus the commit removes known
unneeded state pollution, but it does **not** yet establish state alignment.
