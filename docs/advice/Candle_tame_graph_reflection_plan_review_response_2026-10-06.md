## Overall response

The review is accepted on its two central points.

First, the original witness-carrying backend does not avoid the enumeration it
was meant to avoid. Because every expanded node has its exact successors
recomputed and every successor must be represented, that design still visits
the complete search tree. Quotienting non-final nodes by isomorphism would
require a new equivariance theorem that AFP does not supply and that is not
obviously true for the order-sensitive `minimalFace`/`minimalVertex` choices.
The first production design will therefore have one backend: deterministic
replay of `next_tame`, with checked hints only at final leaves that require an
archive match.

Second, the general pruning/invariant proof is the main project risk and must
start before a complete parameter-0 computation. The roughly 11,000 lines in
the large AFP proof theories are not a literal estimate of HOL Light proof
length, but they are a much better risk indicator than the original list of
nine small omitted theories. A statement-level HOL skeleton and a
line/dependency-weighted proof burndown move into the first work packet.

These corrections supersede the two-backend, duplicate-node, and Stage-3
scheduling sections of the original plan and of
`Candle_tame_graph_parameter0_checker_contract_2026-10-06.md`. They do not
invalidate the authenticated AFP manifest, correspondence inventory, typed
graph encoders, decoder theorems, rejecting validators, or current reflected
data tests. Those are shared prerequisites for the corrected design.

No tame-graph classification or S4 claim follows from this response.

## Corrected trusted architecture

The untrusted input is reduced to an archive-hint stream. A hint used at an
eligible final graph contains:

- an index into the HOL production value `tame_archive_lists` (or a proved
  HOL filter of that value);
- an explicit vertex map; and
- any orientation bit required by the exact `iso_fgraph` checker.

The trusted argument has this shape:

```text
typed HOL next_tame replay
        |
        v
derived/refined cval program
        |
        v
Kernel.compute over seed, fuel, and leaf hints
        |
        v
general HOL replay-soundness theorem
        |
        v
coverage of the production archive
```

For each visited state, the checker has three logical outcomes:

1. **Expanded.** The state is non-final, and the checker computes its exact
   `next_tame p` successors. No external successor list or pruning label is
   accepted.
2. **Final and matched.** The executable filter does not refute the state, so
   the checker consumes a hint and verifies the indicated production archive
   entry and explicit isomorphism map.
3. **Final and rejected.** The executable lower-bound/tameness test rejects
   the state, and a general HOL theorem proves that such a final state cannot
   satisfy `tame`.

The third case is deliberately not called “decided non-tame.” In particular,
`tame13a` is not decided by the executable test. The proof obligation is the
one-way refutation supplied through `untame_negFin` and the lower-bound
argument. Conversely, every final graph not refuted by that machinery is
matched to the archive, even if it is not mathematically tame.

There is no non-final “duplicate” outcome in the first design. There is no
producer-supplied pruning witness. The producer can save final-leaf
isomorphism search and the old trie/hash/worklist bookkeeping, but it cannot
save the deterministic generation and pruning traversal.

The historical 348,231 final visits give an upper scale for hint events, not
an exact final-constant hint count or encoded-size estimate. The actual number
of consumed hints, bytes per hint, and archive-map validation time will be
measured rather than inferred.

## Coverage theorem and chunking

The review's `covers` formulation is the right logical interface. In HOL
Light notation, the target is equivalent to:

```text
covers p A S <=>
  !s g.
    s IN S /\
    (s,g) IN RTranCl (next_plane p) /\
    finalGraph g /\ tame g
    ==> ?a. MEM a A /\ iso_fgraph (fgraph g) a
```

The general proof should provide these rules independently of production
data:

- `covers p A {}`;
- union composition for `covers`;
- coverage of a final state from a checked production-archive map;
- coverage of a final state from a proved executable refutation; and
- coverage of an invariant non-final state when its exact
  `next_tame p` successor set is covered.

The last rule is the HOL destination of the AFP
`filterout_untame_succs`/`filter_tame_succs` and
`next_tame0_comp`/`next_tame_comp` argument. This is the proof spine, not an
after-the-fact interpretation of an already completed computation.

The executable theorem will have a form such as:

```text
replay p A fuel hints stack = SOME remaining_hints
==> covers p A (set stack)
```

with fuel exhaustion, malformed hints, wrong maps, and missing hints all
rejecting. The production acceptance equation will additionally require the
expected hint-consumption condition.

Chunking will be logical and sequential. A proved splitter computes a
frontier inside HOL; chunks cover disjoint sublists of that frontier; the
union rule combines their coverage in the same Candle proof state. Absolute
frontier files, order, counts, and hashes may remain useful diagnostics but
are not soundness premises. Separate Candle processes cannot contribute
independent chunk theorems to one result without a theorem-import mechanism,
which this project does not permit. Checkpoints may preserve the single
kernel state between sequential chunks.

## Scale gate

The corrected Stage 2 has a numeric gate before any full large-partition
replay.

The measurements will include:

1. final-constant node, pruned, final, and hint-consumption counts from an
   instrumented reference run;
2. a complete `p = 0` reflected replay;
3. a deterministic bounded `p = 2` subtree that exercises quadrilateral,
   exceptional-face, nonzero-excess, and multi-size paths;
4. per-node and per-final-hint costs for proposal preparation, cval encoding,
   evaluator execution, and theorem handoff; and
5. the one-time cost of representing the roughly 9 MB production archive
   term.

The full estimate will use the measured final-constant counts and the
representative `p = 2` cost, not just `p = 0` multiplied by 5,980.

A provisional engineering gate is:

- projected sequential evaluator time at most seven days for one production
  replay: proceed to the general-proof/full-partition integration;
- seven to thirty days: perform one bounded, measured representation or
  evaluator optimization and remeasure before proceeding;
- more than thirty days, or an encoded-term/checkpoint size outside the
  agreed machine envelope: stop the full replay and revise the checker
  representation before investing in the complete proof run.

These are scheduling thresholds, not logical assumptions. They include no
credit for parallel proof processes, because their theorem results cannot be
merged under the present trust rules. The thresholds can be revised
explicitly if project priorities justify a longer production replay, but the
estimate and decision must be recorded before parameters 1--3 start.

## Rescheduled proof work

The statement-level HOL skeleton starts now, alongside the data layer. It
will state the final coverage theorem and derive it from named premises for:

- graph/generator invariants;
- correctness of face subdivision and `next_plane`;
- correctness of every executable pruning predicate;
- the lower-bound refutation used for final rejected states;
- preservation of all tame completions by `next_tame`; and
- explicit final-map correctness against production `iso_fgraph`.

Each named premise will be linked to its AFP source theorem, dependency
closure, source line weight, existing HOL leads, and status. The initial
weighting must include at least:

| AFP theory | lines reported by review |
| --- | ---: |
| `FaceDivisionProps` | 4,942 |
| `Invariants` | 2,826 |
| `ScoreProps` | 725 |
| `PlaneProps` | 680 |
| `GeneratorProps` | 537 |
| `GraphProps` | 437 |
| `EnumeratorProps` | 429 |
| `LowerBound` | 297 |
| `TameProps`, `TameEnumProps`, `Plane1Props` | 160 |

Line weight is a triage measure, not a proof estimate. Existing results in
`tame_list.hl` may discharge or shorten parts of this burndown, but that must
be demonstrated theorem by theorem against the constants loaded by the
direct manifest.

The project chooses HOL Light reproof rather than Isabelle-proof transport
for this lane. There is no existing checked Isabelle/HOL-to-HOL-Light theorem
import in the production path; building one would be a separate foundational
and assurance project, and the large computed AFP conclusions themselves
depend on code-runtime definitions and, by default, skipped evaluation.
Isabelle statements and proofs remain valuable specifications and proof
guides. The final authority remains one HOL Light/Candle kernel.

## Executable-specification policy

The executable specification will be defined first as typed HOL list
functions. The cval implementation will be derived from it by refinement,
not maintained as an independent handwritten algorithm.

For the AFP `enum`/`IArray` boundary, the first version will use the logical
`enumerator` function directly. If a list-encoded table is later required for
performance, its equivalence to `enumerator` must be proved.

The present data module writes the logical operation and cval equation by
hand. That remains acceptable for the small representation boundary, but it
will not scale to the executable spine. Before porting roughly one hundred
functions, the lane will prototype a narrow first-order HOL-to-cval
translator/refinement generator. The generator is tooling, not authority:
its generated definitions and refinement theorems must be checked by the HOL
kernel, and unsupported source constructs must fail closed.

## Archive binding and claim direction

Leaf hints will preferably index `tame_archive_lists` directly. If
partition-specific lists are convenient, they will be HOL filters of that
same production value by maximum face size. `Tri.ML`, `Quad.ML`, `Pent.ML`,
and `Hex.ML` then remain authenticated fixtures and differential oracles, not
the logical source of archive membership.

The classification theorem needs only:

```text
enumerated tame final graphs <= archive, up to iso_fgraph
```

The converse direction checked by AFP `samet` is optional consistency
evidence. Archive minimality is not a project goal, and expected partition
counts must not be used to imply it.

## Revised staged order

### A. Historical basis and proof skeleton

- retain the authenticated release manifest and mechanical source inventory;
- correct the baseline discrepancies above;
- generate the weighted, theorem-level pruning/invariant burndown; and
- derive the final `covers` conclusion from explicit named HOL premises.

Exit: the missing general mathematics is enumerable and tied to exact direct
HOL constants. No computation is interpreted as classification.

### B. Data and archive boundary

- retain the existing total encoders/decoders and rejecting validators;
- add canonical rotation/order only where required by the chosen checker;
- prove explicit-map correctness for the exact production `iso_fgraph`; and
- bind the reflected archive representation directly to
  `tame_archive_lists` or its HOL filters.

Exit: authentic archive entries and adversarial mutations pass through one
closed reflected data theorem. This remains a data result.

### C. Typed replay and scale prototype

- define deterministic list-based replay in HOL;
- derive/refine its cval program;
- run complete `p = 0` and a bounded representative `p = 2` subtree;
- regenerate final-constant reference counts; and
- apply the numeric gate above.

Exit: measured feasibility of the actual program that will be proved, not a
different prototype.

### D. General soundness burndown

- discharge the named invariant, pruning, lower-bound, transition, and
  final-map premises;
- prove replay soundness uniformly in parameter, archive, fuel, hint stream,
  and start stack; and
- instantiate it for the complete parameter-0 computation.

Exit: a closed exact parameter-0 classification theorem.

### E. Full partitions and aggregation

- process parameters 1--3 sequentially with logical frontier splitting when
  required;
- combine coverage by proved union rules;
- derive the exact `|- import_tame_classification`; and
- apply it to the existing direct LP/nonlinear result in a clean Candle run.

Exit: the required closed theorem and repeat evidence under the existing
project acceptance rules.

## Immediate changes in direction

The next tame-graph work should therefore be:

1. revise the parameter-0 contract to the single replay-plus-leaf-hints
   design;
2. generate the named-premise `covers` skeleton and weighted proof ledger;
3. keep extending the already valid reflected data/isomorphism boundary;
4. add final-constant reference instrumentation; and
5. define a bounded `p = 2` differential fixture before attempting full
   parameter-0 enumeration infrastructure.

The project should not build a whole-tree witness certificate, prove
non-final isomorphism quotienting, port AFP tries/worklists merely to mimic
`samet`, or start a large reflected replay before the scale and proof-risk
gates are measured.
