## Interpretation

The discriminating experiment narrows the remaining gap:

- aggregate theorem production removes a real 2.35 seconds from this
  eight-box batch, but is not a dramatic speedup by itself;
- the aggregate theorem's non-compute recurring overhead is about 1.92
  seconds: 0.38 seconds of job encoding and 1.54 seconds of logical handoff;
- the 7.11-second computed numerical phase remains the largest recurring
  component;
- the 26.08-second cold preparation remains larger than the complete recurring
  check and therefore must be amortized across useful local groups; and
- exposing the large batch result at the interactive top level added roughly
  2.7 seconds of pure pretty-print/log overhead in a control run.  The actual
  integration should keep these internal results opaque.

This supports the performance note's emphasis on avoiding unnecessary large
intermediate exposure, but it does not show that compact checker output is
expensive.  The checker still returns one Boolean; the observed output cost
was a development-harness artifact from printing its proof record.

The next larger experiment should reuse local-envelope preparation, adaptive
one-split proposals, and the exact tree theorem across a representative real
family.  Arithmetic profiling should continue to focus on the remaining
rational inverse, square-root, and arctangent work inside the measured
7.11-second evaluator phase.
