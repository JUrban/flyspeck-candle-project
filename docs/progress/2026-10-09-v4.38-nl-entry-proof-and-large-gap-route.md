## Keep the two performance gaps separate

The broader performance investigation remains active.  The current evidence
still identifies two distinct problems:

- the matched native-fixed-scale to Candle checked-execution boundary is
  roughly a **15-times** issue on the original comparison; and
- the initial generic-native-fixed-scale to specialized historical C++
  comparison was roughly **340 times**, primarily an architectural warning
  about the amount of derivative/Taylor work, arithmetic choices, and pruning
  strength rather than a prediction for every current fixture.

The latest held-axis factorials assign portions of the numerical gap without
collapsing these figures.  On the complete case10173 batch, changing exact
dyadics to padded hardware intervals gives 1.705 times recurring speedup for
generic whole-box execution and 1.582 times for the historical primitive.
Holding arithmetic fixed and changing the mathematical operation gives 1.557
times exact recurring speedup and changes certificate acceptance from
96/4,173 to 4,173/4,173.  On the fifth-sibling forest, the historical route's
tighter bounds also reduce the complete search from 9,896 to 1,344 visited
nodes.

Those are independent, useful gains, but they are not an orders-of-magnitude
answer.  The mature case10173 compact direct plan remains about 6.4 times
faster recurrently than the historical dense whole-box interpreter.  That is
the strongest current evidence that dense generic jet interpretation should
remain a semantic reference and fallback rather than the throughput target.

## Concrete route for the larger remaining gap

The next implementation target is a checked compact plan, not another scalar
micro-optimization.  The smallest decisive experiment is the authenticated
case16594 plan and genuine fifth-sibling forest:

1. Represent static source slots, support sets, primitive roles, permutations,
   and output assembly as checked data.  Source identity plus role remains the
   dispatch authority; basenames and arbitrary aliases remain rejected.
2. Validate the source DAG to compact plan once, outside the per-box loop.
   Charge this preparation separately and once in batch totals.
3. Execute the complete exact fixed-scale plan in one prepared
   `Kernel.compute` batch, retaining intermediate intervals as data and
   returning a compact verdict plus the audit data required by the soundness
   theorem.
4. Run the same plan and arithmetic in the native harness.  Require identical
   domain outcomes, visited-node order, terminal acceptance, and exact bound
   stream where representations match.  This isolates evaluator,
   representation, allocation, and handoff cost from mathematical work.
5. Compare both against the dense whole-box exact lane and the specialized
   historical native lane.  Record source/plan preparation, recurring
   numerical execution, theorem handoff, complete traversal, peak memory, and
   certificate acceptance separately.
6. Scale only if the complete forest wins materially.  Then repeat on
   case10173, whose 4,173 boxes and distinct 54-instruction source provide the
   existing second-family gate.

The native harness remains the place to test more radical numerical designs
before proving them.  The next such discriminator should hold the compact
plan and genuine boxes fixed while crossing exact checked arithmetic with a
carefully padded native representation.  If that reproduces only the current
roughly 1.6--2.1-times arithmetic gain, effort shifts to stronger specialized
enclosures, sparse derivative propagation, and fewer completed jet operations
rather than low-level encoding tweaks.

Complete-batch time and certificate acceptance remain the deciding measures.
A candidate that is faster per box but loses enough tightness to increase
subdivision does not pass.  Reusable preparation may be expensive, but it is
charged once and must amortize over the real certificate family.
