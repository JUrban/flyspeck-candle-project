## Interpretation of the two gaps

The angle specialization passes its bounded end-to-end gate, but an
approximately two-times prefix improvement is not the target
orders-of-magnitude result.  Its proof remains deferred rather than allowing
one expression-specific optimization to consume the performance programme.

The controlled native experiments already separated the two requested axes
on the same genuine boxes:

- holding the prepared direct graph fixed, exact outward `int128`/dyadic
  endpoints take 0.110726515 seconds for 4173 boxes, versus 0.068002711
  seconds for padded outward `double`: a 1.628-times recurring arithmetic and
  rounding factor;
- holding padded `double`, scale, boxes, and conversion placement fixed, the
  generic deferred graph takes 0.190296989 seconds versus 0.175123190 seconds
  for the direct graph: a 1.087-times recurring derivative/data-flow factor.

Both comparisons accept every box and record bound changes.  On this fixture,
the optimized exact direct native path is now only about 1.35 times slower
than specialized historical C++; the padded-double direct path is faster.
Thus the original approximately 340-times value remains the important
starting generic-native/specialized-C++ gap, but it is no longer the measured
gap for the optimized native prototype.  The remaining large opportunity is
transferring the low-work plan across Candle's encoded evaluator and proof
boundary.  Isolated reflected first- and second-order components still cost
about 34 and 16 times their matched native graphs, respectively.
