## Question

The v2.13 aggregate profile established that the 32 rational-fallback steps
used about 82% of the fixed hybrid's authentic top-level step time.  This
follow-up asks the one remaining useful profiling question: is that cost in
ordinary rational Taylor addition/multiplication or in nonlinear
square-root/inverse/arctangent composition?

The same eight accepted genuine action-296 boxes and exact intermediate
stacks were divided into:

- 22 fixed-polynomial instructions per box;
- 22 rational-algebraic instructions per box: 14 additions and
  8 multiplications; and
- 10 nonlinear-composition instructions per box: 7 square roots,
  1 inverse, 1 arctangent, and 1 pi/2 constant.

Each per-box partition was evaluated twice in an internal force-only batch
returning one bit, and once in an exact batch comparing every computed stack
with its captured value.  All **176/176 polynomial**, **176/176 algebraic**,
and **80/80 nonlinear** steps passed.  The complete rational and fixed
programs also passed 8/8 with exact one-shot agreement, and the axiom set was
unchanged.

The rational additions and multiplications account for about **84.1%** of
the combined algebraic-plus-nonlinear force time in this refined partition.
With equal job counts, an outer rational add/multiply costs about **3.50x**
as much as a fixed-polynomial instruction.  Per instruction it is about
**2.40x** as expensive as the nonlinear group.

Each category total includes eight evaluator entries.  The earlier fixed
equation-table control was about 62 ms per entry, so setup contributes at
most about 0.50 seconds to each total and cannot explain the result.  No
mechanical subtraction is claimed because input translation depends on job
shape.

Exact comparison totals were 19.4234 seconds polynomial, 52.3643 seconds
algebraic, and 7.4989 seconds nonlinear.  The much larger comparison overhead
for algebraic results is further evidence that those dense rational Taylor
values are expensive both to construct and traverse; production correctly
keeps intermediates behind a compact verdict.

## Consequence

This changes the next optimization target.  Another nonlinear primitive or
GCD-fuel micro-optimization is not justified first.  The current hybrid
converts every fixed polynomial block back into the established rational
Taylor-model representation, after which the outer expression performs 22
dense rational additions/multiplications per box.

The promising bounded extension is therefore:

1. preserve the proved fixed-scale Taylor-result invariant across outer
   addition and multiplication;
2. keep those intermediate dense jets in signed fixed-scale form; and
3. convert to the established rational representation only at genuine
   nonlinear boundaries and the final certified interface.

This should reuse the existing fixed-scale invariant and general analytic
soundness theorem rather than introduce a new proof architecture.  Bound
tightness and action-296 acceptance must be checked alongside runtime; a
speedup that creates extra subdivisions is not sufficient.
