## Resolution of the 43-program question

The same captured genuine Flyspeck formula and all 16 exact boxes from its
real adaptive certificate have now been tested through the shared-jet route.
They require **one 87-instruction source program**, not 43 separately expanded
value/gradient/Hessian programs.

The 43-program experiment was a deliberate comparison against the older
explicit-derivative architecture: one value expression, six first
derivatives, and 36 second derivatives. Its preparation blow-up is not a
requirement of the shared-jet architecture and does not justify another
symbolic-differentiation layer.

The shared-jet route already provides the intended universal connection:

1. one source expression is reified and compiled once;
2. the compiled program evaluates its value, gradient, and Hessian jets at
   the center and over each whole box;
3. `candle_q_dim_poly_jet_whole_box_accept_sound` supplies the reusable
   language-wide analytic theorem; and
4. the real-driver adapter transports that theorem to the live certificate
   domain and returns the existing `m_cell_list_pass` interface.

The missing real-data connection was endpoint representation, not
differentiation. The verifier presents each live box using `Arith_float`
terms, while the reflected evaluator consumes exact rational data. The
adapter normalizes those endpoints, checks the one shared source program, and
then transports the accepted theorem back to the original live terms. The
certificate's original split/glue tree and final theorem assembly remain
unchanged.

The exact end-to-end result remains:

```text
programs=1 instructions=87 leaves=16 glues=15
theorem_md5=30400817689af3c5045adfef46e010d9
```

All 16 leaves and 15 glues pass. The final theorem is assumption-free, its
conclusion is syntactically identical to the ordinary real-driver result, and
the global axiom set is unchanged.

## Bottleneck found and bounded correction

The first integration proved the architecture but replayed the 16 leaves in
about 65.65 seconds. An instrumented clean-checkpoint run showed that the
shared-jet source proof took about 1.2--1.6 seconds per leaf, while repeatedly
re-proving two six-component endpoint-vector equalities consumed most of the
remaining time. `Kernel.compute`, certificate glue, source rewriting, and the
final cell handoff were not the bottleneck.

The correction proves two general theorems once: an encoded lower or upper
box selector equals the corresponding vector built by mapping exact-rational
interpretation over any correctly sized box list. Each live leaf now
instantiates those theorems and proves only its six bounded rational endpoint
identities. The former 32 repeated componentwise vector tactic proofs are
gone. Exact left/right term checks and a closed-theorem check remain fail
closed at the transport boundary.

No evaluator, source-language semantics, analytic theorem, expression,
certificate, or final theorem interface changed.
