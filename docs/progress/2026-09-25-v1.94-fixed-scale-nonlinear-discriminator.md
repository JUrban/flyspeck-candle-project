## Question

The independent performance review identified general exact-rational
normalization as a possible remaining cost after the centered Taylor-model
checker had already bounded every completed result to denominator `10^12`.
The v1.92 instruction profile independently counted millions of divisions and
moduli in a genuine batch.

The bounded question was therefore whether the two largest genuine
action-296 polynomial blocks could use signed scaled integers without changing
their rounding boundaries, enclosures, or final concrete results.

## Exact fixed-scale boundary

Every completed polynomial stack node contains:

1. its center value and gradient;
2. its whole-box Hessian;
3. its reconstructed whole-box value; and
4. its reconstructed whole-box gradient bounds.

All of these components are outward-rounded to denominator `10^12` before the
next polynomial instruction. Thus addition can operate directly on signed
scaled integers. Multiplication uses exact integer products followed by the
same directed floor/ceiling at the existing completion boundary. Taylor error
accumulation retains exact powers of the scale and the factor `1/2` until its
final outward rounding.

An ordinary-ML diagnostic evaluator now implements this boundary for
polynomial constants, variables, negation, addition, multiplication, and
squaring. It constructs no theorem and is not trusted. The experiment accepts
the diagnostic result only for comparison and requires the complete concrete
result from every tested block to be structurally identical to
`Kernel.compute`.

## Genuine result

The target blocks are top-level instructions 32 and 33 of the authenticated
action-296 source. They contain 39 and 85 polynomial instructions. Two fresh
checkpoint restores evaluated both blocks over eight distinct accepted
subboxes.

## Interpretation

This is the first positive measurement directly supporting the research
note's arithmetic-representation hypothesis. It is stronger than an informal
floating baseline: on these blocks the signed-scaled algorithm reproduces the
current checker exactly, including all rounded center, gradient, Hessian, and
whole-box components. It therefore introduces no bound widening or additional
subdivision on the tested cases.

It is not yet a Candle speedup. The fast path is ordinary untrusted ML, while
the current comparison executes the generic verified rational program through
`Kernel.compute`. The result justifies the next bounded implementation stage:

1. express the same fixed-scale polynomial operations in Candle's computed
   value language;
2. measure them inside `Kernel.compute` on 1/8/64 distinct genuine boxes;
3. require exact current-output equality and unchanged acceptance/tree shape;
4. only if the in-kernel speedup persists, prove the general fixed-scale
   representation/equivalence invariant and connect it beneath the existing
   universal analytic soundness theorem.

No new per-expression analytic proof is needed. The durable universal
centered-Taylor theorem and genuine split-tree handoff remain the proof
boundary. The next proof obligation is an arithmetic representation theorem,
not another calculus layer.

The two blocks account for approximately 12 seconds of the established
20-second eight-box reflected numerical measurement when called separately.
That makes them a useful first integration target, but call setup differs and
no whole-checker speedup is inferred by simple subtraction.
