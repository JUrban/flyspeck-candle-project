For each source `lin_f` entry it combines an exact signed-integer equality, a
checked basis-selector equality, the already-built tail theorem, and one
generic cons theorem. The final theorem still states the exact equality between
the sparse row denotation and the original source expression.

The first real structural run did not improve the dominant phase: source/number
conversion was 65.70 seconds and the complete sparse terminal was 80.74

## Complete 17-terminal reuse inventory

An authenticated diagnostic reconstructed all 17 real `hard_2.dat` terminals
without proving them and recorded exact variable-basis, normalized source-row,
and weighted-row identities.

The result rules out a simplistic exact-basis cache: all 17 terminals have
different complete variable bases. Their sizes range from 387 to 505 variables.
Normalized source-row overlap is nevertheless substantial. The best pair,
terminals 8 and 10, shares 469 exact source inequalities, or 64.2% of the
smaller row set. Fully weighted rows barely overlap: the best pair in the file
shares 45, and terminals 8 and 10 share only 9. This means source-denotation
preparation is reusable, while multipliers and numerical terminal plans are
genuinely terminal-specific.

A checked reusable context was implemented and tested. It contains:

## External preparation and trust boundary

The measurements support a three-layer design.

### External, untrusted preparation

An external tool may cheaply produce a canonical union basis, sparse row
encodings, signed coefficient encodings, a deduplicated master source-row
table, and per-terminal row-index/multiplier plans. These objects are hints and
must not authorize a theorem by hashes or parsing alone.

### Reusable verification inside Candle

Candle should validate the master table once against the authenticated source
theorem conclusions and prove each exact sparse-row denotation. A generic
selection theorem or checked plan evaluator should then connect a terminal's
row references to the verified table. Reuse must remain tied to the exact
logical basis, source term, and theorem conclusion.

### Genuinely per-terminal work

Candle must freshly validate the selected rows and multipliers, compute the
complete infeasibility verdict, and apply the general soundness theorem. The
result must retain exactly the terminal's original assumptions; a theorem over
the entire master table must not accidentally import assumptions from unrelated
terminals.

The next LP prototype should therefore prepare and verify a master source-row
table once, represent each terminal as an untrusted index/multiplier plan, and
derive the selected terminal theorem through one generic checked-selection
interface. This targets the measured residual costs: per-row cache lookup and
revalidation, 4--6 seconds of `ALL` proof assembly, and repeated construction
of source plans. The existing sparse numerical verdict and soundness theorem
should remain unchanged.
