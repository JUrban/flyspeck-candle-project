## Lazy exact candidate

The candidate gives each internal polynomial node a compact core containing:

- its domain flag;
- its rounded center first jet; and
- its rounded Hessian.

It reconstructs the complete value and gradient bounds at exactly two kinds
of force points:

1. before multiplication or square consumes an operand; and
2. at the polynomial-block boundary.

Multiplication retains the established raw center/Hessian calculation and
rounding. Negation and addition operate on the same center/Hessian fields as
the current evaluator. The final force calls the unchanged established
completion function. Thus the prototype changes evaluation scheduling, not
arithmetic, rounding, enclosure formulas, or theorem interfaces.

## Isolated polynomial result

All 22 authentic blocks were evaluated on each of 128 authenticated genuine
boxes, for 2,816 block/box results. A separate reflected pass compared every
complete candidate result structurally with the established fixed evaluator:

```text
exact_results=2816 assumptions=0 axiom_growth=0
```

All **2,816 / 2,816** results were exactly equal.

| Polynomial-only mode | Run 1 | Run 2 | Mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Established eager completion | 11.484 s | 8.361 s | **9.923 s** |
| Lazy completion | 7.052 s | 6.951 s | **7.002 s** |

The isolated recurring polynomial workload fell by **2.921 seconds**, or
**29.44%**, for a **1.417x** speedup. The exact comparison itself took 18.939
seconds because it deliberately computed both complete results.

Every measured theorem was closed and left the axiom inventory unchanged.
Peak sampled RSS remained 17,982,344 KiB in both runs.

## Proof and integration decision

Unlike the 3.61% shared-error micro-optimization in v3.06, this candidate
removes a structurally dead class of work and produces a reproducible 29.44%
polynomial reduction plus an 8.49% whole-checker reduction. It merits a general
proof, but it is not yet production code.

The intended reusable proof route is:

1. relate a lazy core to the domain, center, and Hessian projections of the
   established complete result;
2. prove that forcing related cores reconstructs the exact established result;
3. prove the postfix step/stack invariant, with multiplication and square
   forcing their operands and retaining identical raw rounding;
4. derive general equality of the lazy and established polynomial programs
   for compiler-produced well-formed programs; and
5. reuse the established fixed-scale and fixed-outer soundness layers rather
   than developing a second analytic theorem.

Production substitution requires that general result plus focused malformed
program/stack rejection tests, the complete 4,173-cell case-10173 replay, and
the existing closed source-theorem handoff. Fixture equality alone is not
sufficient authority.
