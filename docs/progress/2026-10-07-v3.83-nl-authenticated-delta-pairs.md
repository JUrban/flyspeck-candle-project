## Paired source contract

After the successful `4*x_i*delta` radicand experiment, the three matching
39-step payloads remained the largest polynomial class.  Exact symbolic
decoding establishes that they are:

```text
-d(delta)/dx4
-d(delta)/dx5
-d(delta)/dx6
```

The new instruction is coordinate-parameterized.  It obtains the center value
from the corresponding delta-gradient component, its gradient from the
matching row of the delta Hessian, and its constant Hessian from the matching
third-derivative slice.  Source preparation normalizes the exact rational
polynomial and authorizes the instruction only on equality with
`-d(delta)/dx[coordinate]`.  The case16594 contract requires exactly one each
for coordinates 4, 5, and 6.

The factorial holds the already accepted radicand instruction fixed in both
lanes, then changes only these three numerator programs.  It crosses that
choice with the same exact-mixed and padded-hardware backends.

## Bounded proof decision and the larger gap

The combined delta family now justifies a reusable analytic proof packet, but
not a collection of case-specific theorems.  The intended statement is
general over the selected coordinate and should connect:

- exact source-polynomial recognition;
- the delta derivative and `4*x_i*delta` value/gradient/Hessian identities;
  and
- the existing universal jet/Taylor soundness theorem.

Proof work should remain bounded to that family while the whole-chain native
screen runs.  If the whole chain wins, its soundness should be composed from
the same identities and general square-root/inverse/arctangent rules rather
than independently reproving each of three expressions.

The performance accounting remains explicit:

- the historical roughly **340-times** generic-native/specialized-C++ result
  is still a starting architecture comparison on case10173, whose current
  optimized residual is about 1.35 times;
- the case16594 exact/hardware arithmetic difference is now about **2.47
  times** after the two generally useful algorithm instructions; and
- the unchanged 225.8-second reflected batch is now roughly **175 times** the
  1.293-second recurring exact native candidate.  The larger ratio reflects a
  faster native target, not a reflected regression.

Consequently the orders-of-magnitude objective still requires both active
tracks: reusable analytic instructions that cut the work per cell, and the
proved bounded executor/mixed-operation path that removes the formal
execution overhead.  Neither track alone is yet a complete checker result.
