## Question and controlled experiment

The selected exact direct profile attributed 83.15% of recurring evaluation
to the dihedral tangent and Hessian. Those stages used 2,224,209 of the
batch's 2,441,205 semantic interval products. The control always formed all
four endpoint products. The candidate instead uses two endpoint products when
the signs of the two intervals determine the extrema and retains all four
when both intervals straddle zero.

This changes neither the source formula, interval representation, fixed
scale, directed rounding, preparation, Taylor completion, nor acceptance
test. The runner performs twenty warmed, order-balanced repetitions and also
compares the complete result streams from profiled and unprofiled executions.

## Complete 4,173-box result

Every result record is byte-identical between the two lanes, and both accept
4,173/4,173 genuine boxes. Mean measurements were:

| Lane | Preparation | Evaluation | Fully charged batch | Endpoint products |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Four endpoints | 0.412765003 s | 0.123608838 s | 0.536373841 s | 9,731,436 |
| Sign classified | 0.412578306 s | **0.110757078 s** | **0.523335384 s** | **4,882,414** |

The candidate improves recurring evaluation by **1.116 times** and the cold
batch by **1.025 times**. Peak RSS is unchanged at 41,764 KiB. Only two of
2,441,205 semantic interval products required the four-product
both-straddling case; the other products needed two endpoints each.

Stage attribution confirms that the benefit occurs where predicted:

| Stage | Four endpoints | Sign classified | Speedup |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Six coordinate roots | 0.013537176 s | 0.013178912 s | 1.027× |
| Dihedral tangent | 0.033562237 s | 0.030823076 s | 1.089× |
| Dihedral Hessian | 0.066327544 s | 0.056730695 s | 1.169× |
| Angle scale/addition | 0.005330100 s | 0.004884005 s | 1.091× |

The result is a real, broadly reusable arithmetic reduction, but it is not an
architectural answer to the nonlinear cost. Native sign tests and branches
consume part of the saved multiplication cost, while cold preparation
dominates this single-certificate comparison.

## Relation to the earlier reflected sharing loss

This result does not overturn the v1.87 endpoint-sharing experiment. That
experiment moved the same four products through a helper and made 1,024
reflected calls about eight times slower; it did not eliminate two products
using interval signs. The present candidate changes the operation count and
therefore deserves one small `Kernel.compute` discriminator despite the
earlier negative helper result.

The discriminator must compare exact outputs on representative sign patterns
and genuine inputs. Only a measured reflected execution win permits a small
general interval-product lemma and one production integration test. A loss
ends the proof effort. No per-expression specialization follows from this
candidate.

## Keeping both larger gaps explicit

The original performance investigation has two independent boundaries:

1. encoded Candle evaluation was roughly **15 times** slower than an
   equivalent native exact computation;
2. the initial generic native fixed-scale route was roughly **340 times**
   slower than the specialized historical C++ verifier.

The present 1.116-times recurring native improvement is relevant to both
arithmetic implementations but closes neither boundary. Later direct-plan
experiments have greatly reduced the second gap on this fixture, but they are
untrusted native diagnostics and must not be reported as a Candle speedup or
as an orders-of-magnitude full-certificate result.

## Next architectural experiment

After the bounded reflected discriminator, the next native factorial will
hold the complete derivative/Taylor algorithm and genuine boxes fixed while
changing only endpoint arithmetic and rounding. The direct plan already
offers the two matched lanes required for this comparison:

- exact fixed-scale outward endpoints;
- outward hardware-double endpoints with the same prepared direct graph.

It will report preparation, recurring complete-batch time, per-stage
primitive counts, bound containment/tightness, and all 4,173 acceptance
decisions. This isolates arithmetic/rounding from generic derivative and
Taylor work.

The complementary factorial then holds hardware-double arithmetic fixed and
compares the current direct graph with the pinned specialized historical
algorithm, using the same stage boundaries and genuine boxes. That isolates
algorithmic/enclosure work from representation cost. The existing
historical-formula shim is not a substitute: it changed only one formula
inside the generic graph and did not reproduce the specialized data flow.

Complete-batch acceptance and fully charged time decide both gates. A native
winner must generalize to a second certificate family before earning a
generally proved `Kernel.compute` instruction. This keeps the exact-product
proof bounded while directing substantial proof work toward candidates with
orders-of-magnitude potential.
