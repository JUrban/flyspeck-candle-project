## Question

The nonlinear performance investigation has two deliberately separate
starting gaps:

1. about 15 times from the exact-output native computation to its encoded
   `Kernel.compute` execution;
2. about 341 times from the original generic native fixed-scale experiment to
   the historical specialized C++ verifier.

Later untrusted native work has reduced the second ratio substantially on the
current fixture, but that improvement is not yet part of a proof-producing
checker.  The next experiment needed to say whether the historical verifier
wins through tighter Taylor bounds, cheaper derivative/enclosure work, or
final bound aggregation.

## Result 1: almost all historical cost is inside derivative/enclosure work

Seven complete runs gave:

| Historical C++ route | Preparation | Complete evaluation | Domain construction | `evalf` | Final `upperBound` | Accepted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Specialized | 0.000024391 s | 0.083159749 s | 0.000215261 s | 0.080885290 s | 0.001299143 s | 4,173/4,173 |
| Generic composition | 0.000025651 s | 0.324105190 s | 0.000209200 s | 0.321779319 s | 0.001333550 s | 52/4,173 |

The new clocks add small observer overhead, but the totals closely reproduce
the earlier 0.082490265 and 0.326891192 second measurements.  In the
specialized route, `evalf` consumes **97.27%** of complete evaluation while
the final Taylor aggregation consumes only **1.56%**.  Generic `evalf` is
**3.978 times** slower than specialized `evalf`; final aggregation is nearly
unchanged.

This rules out optimizing only the final Taylor sum as an explanation for the
historical gap.  The important target is the low-work construction of center,
gradient, and Hessian/enclosure data.

## Result 2: tighter bounds are not the current graph's limiting problem

On the matched 16-box sample, the current exact fixed-scale generic graph and
its historical-dihedral variant both accept 16/16 boxes.  Relative to the old
specialized C++ route, the current generic graph has:

| Mean component | Current exact fixed graph | Specialized C++ | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Center upper | -0.0042430875 | -0.0042445885 | +0.0000015010 |
| Linear term | 0.0025615330 | 0.0025597061 | +0.0000018269 |
| Quadratic term | 0.0011884075 | 0.0013014078 | **-0.0001130003** |
| Final upper | -0.0004931063 | -0.0003834746 | **-0.0001096317** |

The current fixed graph is therefore about 8.6% tighter in its mean quadratic
term and more negative at the final boundary on this sample.  The specialized
C++ route is faster despite doing slightly looser numerical work here.
Enclosure quality remains a mandatory full-batch gate, but it does not explain
the current specialized route's speed.

Changing only the dihedral implementation inside the fixed graph also fails
the gate.  It leaves the center identical, increases the mean linear term by
0.0000010504, increases the mean quadratic term by 0.0000396594 (3.3%), and
makes the final upper bound 0.0000407098 less negative.  Existing complete
batch measurements likewise make that historical-formula graph slightly
slower.  Formula substitution inside the current theorem-shaped graph is not
a faithful performance port of the historical algorithm.

## Consequence for the larger gaps

The original approximately 15-fold and 341-fold gaps remain useful starting
baselines, but they must not be conflated with the latest untrusted native
candidate.  Successive native experiments have already reduced the numerical
side of the second gap to about 3.9 times for the current exact `int128` graph
and about 2.1 times for the unsafe hardware-`double` lower bound.  None of
those improvements yet removes the encoded-execution or theorem-handoff cost
inside Candle.

The new result identifies the next factorial cleanly: reproduce the old
specialized derivative/enclosure data flow, not merely its final bound or its
dihedral formula.

## Bounded next work packet

1. Use the pinned specialized C++ stage records as an oracle and port its
   six-coordinate center/gradient/Hessian path to a small POD hardware-double
   implementation in the native harness.  Preserve its interval operations,
   table choices, and one complete Taylor handoff rather than routing through
   the generic expression graph.
2. First require agreement at all exported stage boundaries on the 16 boxes.
   Then require full-batch acceptance, bound containment, preparation time,
   recurring time, cold total, and peak memory on all 4,173 boxes.
3. If the POD port approaches the C++ time, hold that algorithm fixed and
   replace its scalar operations with exact outward dyadic operations.  This
   is the arithmetic-representation factorial still missing from the current
   evidence.
4. If the exact crossing remains practical, expose one authenticated
   complete-box instruction under the existing universal analytic soundness
   theorem and measure it in `Kernel.compute`.  This is where the separate
   approximately 15-fold encoded-execution gap is tested.
5. Repeat only after a second expression/certificate family is available;
   complete-batch acceptance and amortized throughput, not a single leaf,
   decide whether the architecture scales.

The existing exact-bound specialization proof remains bounded to its current
small reusable lemma and one integration test.  It may be retained if it
closes cheaply or is used by the complete-box instruction, but no additional
per-expression derivative or Taylor proof layer will be built before the
native full-batch gate demonstrates a substantial recurring win.

This preserves the broader orders-of-magnitude objective while avoiding proof
investment in the already rejected limb representation, final-sum-only
optimization, or historical-formula shim.
