## The research note identified a measured preparation bottleneck

The v1.85 triage proposed a discriminating test on the genuine action-296
checker. The new scaling fixture prepares the authenticated source and
whole-box square-root certificates once, then checks exact subdivisions of
one real accepted action-296 cell:

- one box: the fixed accepted probe root;
- eight boxes: three complete exact split levels at coordinates 1, 2, and 3;
- sixty-four boxes: six complete exact split levels at coordinates 1 through
  6.

The boxes are distinct; they are not repeated copies used to make a favorable
batch. Each size is submitted as one internal reflected batch. Empty batches
with the identical equation set are measured before and after as a bounded
fixed-setup control. Candle's clock remains deterministic zero, so the
existing external observer timestamps flushed phase markers and samples the
actual restored proof PID.

The first complete baseline accepted all **73** cells and produced these wall
times:

| Phase | 1 box | 8 boxes | 64 boxes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Per-box center-variant preparation | 4.732 s | 39.971 s | 340.876 s |
| `Kernel.compute` | <0.05 s | 17.113 s | 161.366 s |
| Complete checked batch | 0.002 s | 18.331 s | 180.545 s |

Shared whole-box source preparation cost 5.006 seconds. Both empty-batch
controls were below the observer's resolution. That control is deliberately
not subtracted mechanically: the empty checker short-circuits before a real
job and is only evidence that equation-set/fixed-call overhead is not the
large cost seen in this fixture. The nearly free one-box compute is also not
used as a general throughput estimate; the eight- and sixty-four-box cases
are the informative slopes.

The baseline resolves the note's main ambiguity. Per-box preparation was
larger than reflected execution: 340.876 seconds versus 161.366 seconds at 64
boxes. This was not repeated analytic-library loading, memory pressure, full
diagnostic conversion, or repeated `Kernel.compute` setup.

## Narrow correction: stop proving discarded rational proposals

Point square-root hint preparation previously substituted exact center values
and called `REAL_RAT_REDUCE_CONV` for each square-root argument. It retained
only the reduced rational and discarded the theorem. The reflected checker
then independently authenticated the proposed square-root interval before it
could contribute to any theorem.

The preparation lane now evaluates supported rational source syntax directly
with exact `Num` arithmetic. It supports rational constants and variables,
negation, addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, inverse, and
natural powers. The six-variable environment is constructed once per
evaluation rather than at every syntax node. The old proof-producing callback
is retained as a development oracle.

This does not move trust out of Candle or weaken the analytic theorem:

1. the new arithmetic is untrusted certificate preparation;
2. every resulting endpoint remains data to the unchanged reflected checker;
3. the same universal centered-Taylor soundness theorem consumes only checked
   acceptance; and
4. the source-erasure and exact split-tree contracts are unchanged.

A nine-case focused regression compared the new evaluator with kernel rational
reduction across every supported operation and passed without changing the
axiom set. On a genuine action-296 box, all seven proposed square-root
intervals also matched the legacy proof-producing oracle exactly.

## Measured improvement and repeat

Two fresh optimized 1/8/64 restores accepted all 73 cells:

| Run | Phase | 1 box | 8 boxes | 64 boxes |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| optimized | preparation | 2.130 s | 15.273 s | 126.661 s |
| optimized | `Kernel.compute` | <0.05 s | 16.288 s | 154.273 s |
| optimized | checked batch | 0.002 s | 17.415 s | 172.592 s |
| repeat | preparation | 2.130 s | 15.426 s | 128.243 s |
| repeat | `Kernel.compute` | <0.05 s | 16.237 s | 156.302 s |
| repeat | checked batch | 0.002 s | 17.364 s | 174.673 s |

At 64 boxes, preparation improved from 340.876 seconds to 126.661--128.243
seconds, or **2.66--2.69 times faster**. Preparation plus the complete checked
batch improved from 521.421 seconds to 299.253--302.916 seconds, or
**1.72--1.74 times faster**: a **41.9--42.6% wall-time reduction**. The sampled
peak remained 2,253,704 KiB in the baseline and both optimized profiles.

The numerical kernel was intentionally unchanged. Its 154--156 second repeat
range versus the 161 second baseline is ordinary run variation, not claimed as
a checker optimization.

## What the finer preparation split says

With phase endpoints moved inside one preparation function so interactive
printing is excluded, eight optimized boxes divide as follows:

| Preparation subphase | Wall time | Share of measured preparation subphases |
|---|---:|---:|
| Exact square-root hint generation | 10.383 s | 68.9% |
| Certificate AST replacement | 0.062 s | 0.4% |
| Proof-producing program compilation | 3.777 s | 25.1% |
| Reflected program encoding | 0.847 s | 5.6% |

The one-box legacy-oracle comparison, which runs both the old and new
calculations and checks exact agreement, cost 5.635 seconds.

This materially narrows the next choices:

- The research note was correct that discarded proof-producing rational
  reduction mattered; removing it accounts for the measured 2.66--2.69x
  preparation speedup.
- AST replacement is not worth optimizing now.
- The remaining hint cost is the largest preparation component. A bounded
  next experiment should precompile or cache the seven square-root argument
  expression evaluators once per source expression, then evaluate only their
  exact data at each center. This preserves their treatment as untrusted
  proposals.
- Per-box proof-producing compilation is now the second preparation cost.
  Longer term, the checker interface could accept per-box certificate data
  separately from the shared source program, with a general erasure/patching
  theorem, instead of compiling the full certificate-decorated program for
  every box.
- The reflected numerical kernel is now again the largest single recurring
  phase. Rational normalization/GCD, dense jet completion, and sparse/constant
  short circuits remain suitable measured targets. The current evidence does
  not yet choose among them.
- For 64 boxes, the checked-batch wrapper adds about 18 seconds beyond
  `Kernel.compute`. Job encoding and final proof linking therefore remain
  visible but smaller than either numerical execution or per-box preparation.
