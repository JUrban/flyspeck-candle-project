## Question and boundary

The native factorials have already separated arithmetic width from numerical
data flow on all 4,173 genuine case-10173 boxes. They show that neither the
1.628-times exact-versus-padded-double difference nor the 1.087-times
generic-versus-direct difference explains the remaining proof-producing cost.

The next question is where that cost begins inside Candle. Before porting the
historical dihedral tangent and Hessian, this packet establishes a lower bound
at the immediately later boundary. Native preparation supplies each completed
center interval, six radii, six center-gradient absolute bounds, and 21
symmetric Hessian absolute bounds. `Kernel.compute` then performs only the
final rounded Taylor completion and returns one upper bound per box.

This deliberately does **not** constitute the proposed direct checker:
`dihedral_inside_compute=0` is part of the success marker. It is a controlled
way to decide whether final Taylor assembly and theorem handoff deserve more
specialization before the expensive derivative engine is moved.

## Interpretation and the two performance gaps

The original approximately **15-times** Candle/equivalent-native encoded gap
and approximately **340-times** generic-native/specialized-C++ starting gap
remain useful, distinct baselines. Native experiments have reduced the latter
to a small factor for this one optimized, untrusted direct plan. They have not
made that plan authoritative inside Candle.

This lower bound now rules out final Taylor aggregation as the main formal
bottleneck. Even a linear full-batch extrapolation of the accepted complete
lane is only about 7.65 seconds of Kernel computation, versus 434.36 seconds
for the current complete numerical/topology verdict. That extrapolation is
diagnostic rather than a claimed full-batch result, but it is sufficient to
reject another final-sum specialization. The large remaining formal cost must
be sought earlier, principally in production of the tangent, gradient, and
Hessian data and in their encoded execution.

The current exact-bound specialization proof remains bounded. No additional
proof layer is justified by this single-fixture late-stage result.

## Next bounded experiment

The next experiment will hold exact decimal fixed-scale representation,
rounding, genuine boxes, and the historical direct formula fixed while moving
only the dihedral tangent/Hessian boundary:

1. port the authenticated historical dihedral tangent and Hessian to the
   existing `cval` fixed-scale substrate;
2. begin with one and then 16 genuine boxes, initially consuming sealed
   prevalidated square-root enclosures so that angle/derivative evaluator cost
   is measured independently of root generation;
3. compare the exact stage outputs and operation boundaries with the matched
   native exact direct implementation;
4. if that gate is numerically exact and materially promising, bring the root
   calculation inside and run the complete 128-box direct call;
5. advance to 4,173 boxes and a second certificate family only after the
   128-box route preserves acceptance and materially improves total prepared
   compute plus handoff cost.

This design holds arithmetic representation fixed while changing numerical
data flow, and its matched native execution holds the algorithm fixed while
exposing evaluator overhead. It therefore separates the two effects requested
without starting a general proof prematurely. Only a full-batch, cross-family
winner earns the reusable complete-box soundness theorem.
