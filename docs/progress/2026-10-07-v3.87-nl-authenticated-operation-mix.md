## Why the trace ledger was refreshed

The v3.86 word benchmark conservatively projected the cost of the 32,672,500
interval endpoint products in the accepted authenticated-delta candidate.  A
complete executor trace must also charge the scalar products and
accumulations surrounding those interval operations.  It must model actual
sign branches and directed-rounding behavior rather than a synthetic mix.

The native source's existing range profiler was therefore extended with
three diagnostic counts only:

- total interval-addition endpoints;
- the nine ordered nonnegative/nonpositive/mixed interval-product sign
  classes; and
- negative quotient numerators and nonzero quotient remainders.

The instrumentation does not change arithmetic, dispatch, bounds, or
acceptance.  A checked-256 profiling build ran the same authenticated
radicand/derivative plan over all 875 genuine case16594 cells.  Its 875 bound
records are byte-identical to the accepted mixed-128/192 lane.

## Corrected cost screen

A conservative full product ledger charges:

- 32,672,500 interval endpoint products;
- 12,789,000 scalar dot products;
- 976,500 wide weighted products;
- 1,953,000 gradient-bound products; and
- four product-equivalents per Taylor completion.

That totals **49,042,000 product-equivalents**.  Charging every one at the
v3.86 measured 155.43 ns product-plus-`DIV 2^40` cost projects **7.62
seconds**.  This deliberately overcharges products that need no immediate
shift, yet remains below the 22.58-second recurring budget for a tenfold
reduction from the current 225.8-second reflected batch.

This is still not a checker-speed claim.  The remaining budget must cover
the genuine sign-class distribution, signed comparisons, 128/192 additions
and subtraction, carries, 11.664 million directed shifts, successful
narrowing, explicit overflow/fallback, batch conversion, and compact theorem
handoff.  The next compiled trace will use the counts above rather than the
earlier endpoint-product-only projection.
