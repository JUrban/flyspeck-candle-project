## Fixed/rational transition discriminator

With the scheduler understood correctly, the remaining representation
question is whether transitions at nonlinear boundaries are themselves
material. A new probe ran the existing fixed-outer checker on the same first
128 authenticated case-10173 jobs in five consecutive calls:

1. the unchanged established checker;
2. a branch-identical control that checks each operand constructor;
3. two probe calls that deliberately repeat every fixed-to-rational or
   rational-to-fixed conversion selected by the authentic instruction and
   stack state; and
4. a second branch-identical control.

The probe runner still executes every authentic fixed-outer instruction and
requires final acceptance. Its additional conversions are diagnostic only.
Every result was the same closed acceptance value and the axiom inventory was
unchanged.

| Call | Wall seconds |
|---|---:|
| Established checker | 21.461 |
| Constructor control 1 | 21.763 |
| Repeated conversions 1 | 22.671 |
| Repeated conversions 2 | 20.453 |
| Constructor control 2 | 22.069 |

The two controls averaged **21.916 seconds**. The two duplicated-conversion
calls averaged **21.562 seconds**, 1.61% lower rather than higher. The full
range across the four probes was 20.453--22.671 seconds, so the small mean
difference is ordinary call variation, not a speedup.

Repeating all current transition conversions therefore adds no measurable
cost on this genuine prefix. Fixed/rational conversion machinery is not a
material explanation of the remaining complete-checker time. This experiment
does not isolate the arithmetic inside inverse, square root, or arctangent,
nor the fixed Taylor operations surrounding them.

Peak sampled RSS was 17,982,344 KiB. The success marker records 128 accepted
cells, two controls, two conversion probes, closed results, and zero axiom
growth.
