## Performance-note assessment

The source review in
`docs/advice/Candle_NL_Performance_Research_2026-09-25.md` has been useful,
but later measurements distinguish the hypotheses sharply:

- **Confirmed material:** exact-rational normalization and arithmetic in the
  algebraic prefix.  The proved fixed-scale hybrid reduced the genuine
  64-box `Kernel.compute` phase from 161.3204 to 63.0574 seconds (**2.56x**)
  and the complete theorem-producing batch from 180.7978 to 83.9137 seconds
  (**2.15x**).
- **Already addressed:** one internal multi-box compute call, compact verdicts,
  compiled point expressions shared per source, ordinary exact generation of
  untrusted square-root hints, universal analytic soundness, certificate
  erasure, and exact split-tree theorem assembly.
- **Measured secondary or negative:** GCD fuel representation, constant-node
  completion, explicit sharing of interval endpoint products, and a proved
  Hessian-only multiplication projection.  None explains the remaining
  minute-scale cost; two proposed rewrites made no improvement or regressed.
- **Still credible and worth discriminating:** rational inverse, square root,
  arctangent and rational/fixed conversion; dense list/pair traversal and
  allocation; per-box certificate/variant preparation; and, above all,
  acceptance coverage and any additional subdivisions caused by looser
  enclosures.
- **Not currently implicated:** repeated evaluator setup.  Constant controls
  measured about 40 ms or below the 0.05-second observer resolution, far below
  the genuine 64-box compute time.

The next performance experiment should therefore use several distinct real
boxes, charge shared expression/plan preparation once, record acceptance and
extra subdivision as well as wall time, and profile accepted cells by
top-level instruction.  A shared-source/data-only certificate interface is a
reasonable prototype only after per-box preparation is split into certificate
generation, theorem construction, program encoding, and job encoding.
