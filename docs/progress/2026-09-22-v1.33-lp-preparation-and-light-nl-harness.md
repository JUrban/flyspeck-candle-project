## LP: what may safely be prepared outside Candle

The indexed master prototype establishes a useful three-part boundary.

External, untrusted preparation may produce:

- a canonical variable table;
- deduplicated sparse source-row encodings;
- integer indices into that table; and
- per-terminal multiplier/evaluation plans.

Reusable work inside Candle must authenticate every master row against the
exact source theorem and produce the weight-general denotation theorem.  Each
terminal must still check every supplied index against its exact source
conclusion, specialize its own multiplier, run the complete numerical
verdict, and cross the generic soundness boundary.  Neither an external index
nor a hash authorizes theorem content by itself.

The non-perturbing real terminals 8/10 profile measured master-source
deduplication at **0.31 seconds** and the interleaved authenticated row-theorem
phase at **86.31 seconds**, for **86.62 seconds** total master preparation.
The latter is close to the earlier v5 measurement of 84.57 seconds. Therefore
moving deduplication or indexing outside Candle is useful for reuse and input
formatting but cannot by itself solve cold cost. The next material target is a
proved computed table-correspondence check or another way to batch source-row
authentication without reconstructing 1,017 host proofs.

## Two bounded negative LP experiments

A one-shot pure expansion of the complete `ALL` row predicate preserved the
exact terminal theorems but was decisively slower. The row-list phase grew
from 5.13/5.38 seconds to 54.47/51.69 seconds, and complete computed terminals
grew to 68.75/66.15 seconds. The change was reverted completely. The negative
run is `cv-lp-sparse-hard2-batch8-10-v6`; log SHA-256
`7f36390201a07d914c39c88c7114a135ea65f93028eaa2910dfa9bf01942055d`
and profile SHA-256
`8d1eca5295f3101b7e4c3af02646ea49eca383c8339d897748d0512a206f0645`.

An attempted two-pass split of master authentication and generalized theorem
construction also perturbed the workload: retaining all authenticated
intermediates before theorem construction raised master time to 95.92 seconds.
Its apparent 84.55-second authentication and 11.06-second theorem split is not
a neutral decomposition. The production schedule was restored to interleave
the two operations. This avoids a misleading optimization based on changed
proof-object lifetime or GC behavior.

## Nonlinear: reusable lightweight experiment state

The existing `cv-staged-base-checkpoint-v2` is now exposed through a small
fresh-restore harness on isolated branch
`codex/flyspeck-cv-nonlinear-harness-v1`, commit `c6f32fd4`. It sits after HOL
startup, arithmetic-float support, the staged polynomial checker, and small
Taylor fixtures, but before experiment-specific code. Every invocation makes
a fresh read-only reflink copy, records exact checkpoint/driver identities,
rejects error markers, and requires an explicit success marker. It never
continues from a failed or partially loaded experiment.

## Next nonlinear separation

The lightweight state is appropriate for numerical representation, whole
polynomial/Taylor arithmetic plans, and theorem-handoff experiments using
captured exact fixtures. It does not contain certificate search, genuine
Taylor theorem construction, subdivision, or root reconstruction. Once the
native or full-verifier lane produces a genuine first-leaf
first-derivative/Hessian pair, those exact inputs should be frozen as the next
fixture and iterated here. A coherent candidate must still pass from a fresh
full nonlinear closure before it can support an integration claim.
