## Research-note assessment

The source review in
`docs/advice/Candle_NL_Performance_Research_2026-09-25.md` is useful, but its
candidate explanations now have different evidential weights.

Several recommendations were already present in the current architecture:

- one compiled source program and shared jets replace separately expanded
  derivative programs;
- stable analytic libraries and reflected support are checkpointed;
- the genuine benchmark reuses source preparation across eight distinct
  accepted boxes;
- internal batches return a compact count or verdict rather than all
  intermediate Taylor data; and
- fixed-scale polynomial evaluation already avoids exact-rational GCD work.

The note's rational-normalization diagnosis remains the most relevant.  The
v2.14 profile located the main authentic step cost in rational algebraic
composition, rather than the nonlinear primitives.  The new add/multiply
split makes that diagnosis more precise.  Across the same eight boxes it
measured 112 rational additions and 64 rational multiplications per force
pass.  Mean force time was:

| Category | Jobs | Mean total | Mean/job |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Rational addition | 112 | 22.4287 s | 200.26 ms |
| Rational multiplication | 64 | 9.5919 s | 149.87 ms |

Thus additions accounted for about **70.0%** of rational add/multiply force
time, and one dense result addition was about **1.34x** as expensive as one
multiplication in this workload.  All exact stack comparisons passed, so the
partition measures the authentic captured instructions rather than a synthetic
arithmetic loop.

This does not show that GCD fuel construction is individually unimportant.
It does show that profiling and bypassing a whole demonstrated class of dense
rational operations is a better next step than optimizing the fuel mechanism
in isolation.  Likewise, constant special cases, unused full-jet components,
and shared endpoint products remain plausible secondary experiments after the
current proof bridge.  Repeated evaluator setup and proof-producing hint
preparation are already amortized or excluded from this particular inner-step
comparison; they still matter for complete cold and per-certificate timings.

## Fixed-addition discriminator

A bounded prototype now keeps the established behavior through all nonlinear
instructions and rational multiplication/square operations.  Only negation
and addition after the final nonlinear instruction use the already proved
fixed-scale result operations.  Arbitrary rational results are outwardly
rounded at that boundary, and the final result is converted back to the
established certified interface.

An early version incorrectly passed rational-encoded radii to operations that
require signed fixed-scale radii.  Its loose bounds and failed boxes were a
prototype representation error, not an architectural result.  After using
the proved rational-to-fixed radii conversion, two fresh runs gave:

| Run | Established hybrid | Fixed-addition tail | Reduction | Accepted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| v13 | 8.2316 s | 6.9110 s | 16.0% | 8/8 vs 8/8 |
| v14 repeat | 7.3575 s | 5.5421 s | 24.7% | 8/8 vs 8/8 |

Using the mean times across both runs gives **7.7946 s versus 6.2266 s**, a
**20.1% reduction**.  Both computed results are assumption-free, the axiom set
is unchanged, and all eight boxes are genuine action-296 accepted boxes with
shared preparation.  This is numerical development evidence, not yet a
theorem-producing checker result.

## Consequence and next proof boundary

This is the first positive authentic-box evidence that replacing part of the
dense rational outer algebra can improve the reflected checker without losing
bound tightness or increasing subdivisions.  It supports completing a narrow
soundness bridge rather than starting another broad numerical redesign:

1. prove that outward conversion of an arbitrary rational Taylor result to the
   fixed representation preserves its value, gradient, and Hessian
   enclosures;
2. state the tagged-item invariant in terms of the existing universal analytic
   invariant after conversion back to the rational interface;
3. prove fixed negation/addition preserve that invariant in the post-nonlinear
   tail; and
4. replay the genuine eight-box theorem production through the certified
   interface and compare its theorem digest with the established result.

Multiplication remains rational for now.  Extending fixed-scale multiplication
is justified only after the smaller addition bridge is proved and measured in
the complete proof path.  The other research-note experiments remain queued by
measured priority: full-jet projection and constant reconstruction first if
the fixed tail leaves substantial numerical cost, then evaluator setup/output
and untrusted hint preparation in complete-certificate profiles.
