## Question and boundary found

The bounded-compute packet asks whether the reflected/native execution gap can
be attacked by keeping exact fixed-width arithmetic as Word64 limbs inside a
verified executable path.  The first attempt tried to translate HOL4's generic
`add_with_carry`.  The translator rejected it, and inspection showed why: its
definition crosses through `w2n` and `n2w`.  Merely persuading that function to
translate would therefore preserve the conversion cost that this experiment
is meant to remove.

The replacement is a small Word64-only carry primitive.  It uses word
addition, unsigned word comparison, word equality, Boolean disjunction, and a
pair result.  A general HOL theorem proves it equal to the existing
`add_with_carry` result.  The already proved checked 128- and 192-bit additions
now use this primitive and retain their exact refinement theorems and explicit
overflow result.

## Verified translation result

CakeML's verified translator accepted:

1. the Word64-only carry primitive;
2. checked two-limb/128-bit addition; and
3. checked three-limb/192-bit addition.

The focused build completed in **81.56 seconds** at **1,726,816 KiB** maximum
RSS.  The translation log records the three generated value theorems
`bounded_word64_add_carry_v_thm`, `bounded128_add_words_v_thm`, and
`bounded192_add_words_v_thm`.

This is an execution-representation milestone, not a speed claim.  It proves
that the verified CakeML executable can retain word operations without
falling back through unbounded naturals.  It does not yet place the new values
inside Candle's `Kernel.compute` datatype or measure recurring execution and
theorem handoff.

## Bounded next gate and broader plan

The next packet remains deliberately small: translate a closed repeated
addition workload, compile or execute the verified program, and charge
conversion, recurring work, result validation/theorem handoff, and overflow
fallback separately.  The exact refinement theorem must cover the repeated
wrapper rather than relying on a separately retyped benchmark.

Only a measured win opens the next packet: word-level wide multiplication and
directed shifts, followed by the complete 875-box reflected gate.  A loss
stops this datatype specialization.

The broader two-axis investigation is unchanged:

- the approximately 15-times Candle/native gap is primarily an execution and
  representation question; and
- the historical approximately 340-times generic-native/specialized-C++ gap
  is primarily an algorithm, arithmetic-choice, and enclosure-work question.

The paired check-removal experiment already bounded dynamic checking at about
1.012 times, so no large range-proof packet will be undertaken merely to
remove checks.  Reusable source-derived numerical instructions remain the
larger-gap route.  The bounded-word work is valuable only if it supplies a
cheap proved substrate for those algorithms.
