## General proof chain

The lazy completion optimization is no longer justified only by fixture
equality. The general proof now establishes, without new axioms, that:

1. for every compiler-produced polynomial AST and arbitrary logical center
   boxes and radii, the lazy fixed-scale evaluator returns exactly the
   established fixed-scale result;
2. substituting that evaluator at polynomial instructions leaves the complete
   fixed-outer program and certified-check result unchanged for compiled
   analytic expressions;
3. the guarded raw variable-certificate batch returns exactly the established
   raw verdict for every compiler-produced authenticated source and every raw
   certificate value; and
4. pairing that numerical verdict with the unchanged compact topology checker
   leaves the complete one-verdict checker unchanged.

The proof deliberately does not authorize arbitrary malformed postfix source
programs. The production boundary is the authenticated compiler-produced
source plus the existing canonical raw-certificate guard. Unexpected raw job
representations continue to fail that guard.

The complete lazy checker inherits the established analytic, topology, root,
and source soundness theorems by proved equality. No duplicate calculus or
tree-soundness architecture was introduced.

## Interpretation

Lazy completion is a sound, reusable improvement, not the large architectural
speedup sought for the full nonlinear workload. The full-scale 12% numerical
gain is stronger than the 128-cell 8.49% sample and shows that the saving does
not disappear with scale. It also confirms that the optimization introduces
no rational-growth or memory regression.

The result does not change the larger diagnosis: repeated source/certificate
preparation and remaining rational arithmetic still dominate the gap to fast
numerical verification. Further work should retain this proved evaluator while
continuing the stable-source/data-only-certificate separation and measured
arithmetic work described in the architecture response.
