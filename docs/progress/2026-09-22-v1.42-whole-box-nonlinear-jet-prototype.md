## Radical whole-box architecture

A bounded two-variable polynomial prototype now begins with one authenticated
postfix representation of the original expression and an exact rational box.
One `Kernel.compute` invocation performs all recurring numerical work:

1. derives the exact midpoint and radii from the box;
2. evaluates the expression at the midpoint;
3. propagates first derivatives with forward-mode interval jets;
4. propagates all three second-derivative/Hessian entries over the box;
5. checks the box ordering;
6. forms the complete second-order Taylor upper bound; and
7. returns an acceptance bit and the exact bound.

Intermediate values, intervals, derivatives, and Hessian entries remain data.
The checker does not consume six numerical theorems made by the legacy
evaluator. This tests the requested change in where computation begins rather
than merely accelerating a final sum over previously proved bounds.

The fixture is

```text
f(x,y) = x^3 + x*y^2 - 1
box    = [0,1/4] x [0,1/4]
```

It has a genuinely varying Hessian. The authenticated source compiles to 12
postfix instructions. The complete reflected check accepts and returns the
exact upper bound

```text
-123/128
```

## Proof boundary reached

The prototype is proof-producing through the arithmetic and automatic
differentiation boundary:

- the cval evaluator is proved equal to an ordinary exact-interval jet
  algorithm;
- the interval-jet algorithm is proved to enclose a real forward-mode
  value/gradient/Hessian semantics for every supported program;
- the original expression reifier produces a hypothesis-free theorem for the
  authenticated 12-instruction program;
- the `Kernel.compute` result is combined with the generic checker
  representation theorem to derive a hypothesis-free HOL theorem that the
  ordinary whole-box checker accepted; and
- the computation, representation, enclosure, reifier, and acceptance
  theorems all retain empty hypothesis lists.

The remaining mathematical bridge is explicit and important: prove once that
the real jet semantics generated from an authenticated source expression is
the expression's value, partial derivatives, and second partial derivatives,
then feed those enclosures to the loaded Flyspeck Taylor soundness theorem.
Until that bridge produces the original inequality theorem, this is not a
complete nonlinear leaf proof and is not evidence for S3 closure.

Commit `fea08a2` now states that bridge as an explicit
`candle_real_jet_taylor_contract`: an original function must be bounded on the
box by the reflected center value, reflected first derivatives, and one
in-box reflected Hessian remainder.  Proved midpoint-containment, radius
nonnegativity, and box-deviation lemmas support the eventual handoff.  A fresh
from-zero replay passed after this addition.  The contract deliberately does
not pretend to prove the calculus obligation; it makes the remaining reusable
source-family obligation auditable while keeping recurring numerical work as
checker data.

### Universal theorem target

The contract is an intermediate specification, not a calculus obligation to
be discharged separately for thousands of expressions.  The intended target
is one structural theorem for every valid expression in the supported
language.  In schematic HOL notation:

```text
CANDLE_EXPR_JET_CORRECT:
  valid_expr e /\ domain_ok e box
  ==>
    diff2c_domain box (denote e) /\
    (!z. z IN box
         ==> jet_f   (real_jet_eval e z) = denote e z /\
             jet_fx  (real_jet_eval e z) = partial 1 (denote e) z /\
             jet_fy  (real_jet_eval e z) = partial 2 (denote e) z /\
             jet_fxx (real_jet_eval e z) = partial2 1 1 (denote e) z /\
             jet_fxy (real_jet_eval e z) = partial2 1 2 (denote e) z /\
             jet_fyy (real_jet_eval e z) = partial2 2 2 (denote e) z)
```

For the current polynomial fragment, `domain_ok` is trivial.  When division,
square root, logarithm, inverse trigonometric functions, and similar operators
are added, it records the exact side conditions checked by the reflected
domain checker.  Each language constructor needs one compositional proof;
each expression does not.

Combining that theorem once with Flyspeck's Taylor theorem should produce the
generic bridge:

```text
CANDLE_EXPR_TAYLOR_CONTRACT:
  valid_expr e /\ domain_ok e (box ix iy) /\ box_valid ix iy /\
  compile e = program
  ==> candle_real_jet_taylor_contract
        program ix iy (uncurry (denote e))
```

The end-to-end reusable soundness theorem can then have the shape:

```text
CANDLE_WHOLE_BOX_ACCEPT_SOUND:
  valid_expr e /\ domain_ok e (box ix iy) /\ box_valid ix iy /\
  compile e = program /\
  candle_q_jet_whole_box_accept program ix iy
  ==> !z. z IN box ix iy ==> denote e z < &0
```

Per Flyspeck formula, the remaining work should be mechanical and
hypothesis-free where possible:

1. reify the original HOL formula to `e` and prove that its denotation is the
   original formula;
2. reify its box and any operator-domain certificate; and
3. instantiate the universal theorem with the accepted computed result.

There should be no hand-written derivative or Taylor-remainder proof per
formula.  If the implementation begins accumulating such proofs, that is a
design regression rather than the intended staging.

### Recommended proof route

The universal analytic proof should be over a small in-logic expression AST,
not over arbitrary HOL syntax and not directly over potentially ill-stacked
postfix bytecode.  Define, inside HOL:

- `denote : expr -> real^N -> real`;
- `valid_expr : num -> expr -> bool`, including variable bounds;
- `domain_ok : expr -> box -> bool` (or a certificate predicate for the
  non-polynomial operators);
- exact real jet semantics and exact-interval jet semantics; and
- `compile : expr -> instruction list`.

Structural induction on `expr` then proves value/gradient/Hessian correctness
once per constructor.  A separate compiler theorem connects the AST jet to the
existing reflected postfix evaluator.  This separation prevents stack
underflow defaults from entering the calculus theorem and gives domain side
conditions a natural compositional home.

The production language should be dimension-parametric, with the current
two-variable six-component jet retained only as the bounded architectural
spike.  Flyspeck leaves normally use six variables, so proving a large second
fixed-width calculus layer would create avoidable rework.  The reflected data
representation can use lists or fixed-size indexed data after profiling; that
choice need not change the analytic theorem.

The staged proof plan is:

1. define the polynomial AST and prove the universal jet theorem for constants,
   variables, negation, addition, multiplication, and natural powers;
2. compile the existing cubic fixture through the AST and derive its complete
   original-function box theorem, closing the prototype's current analytic
   gap;
3. make the jet dimension-parametric and instantiate the theorem at Flyspeck's
   real dimension;
4. connect the generic jet theorem once to Flyspeck's existing
   `partial`/`partial2` and multivariate Taylor result;
5. add real operators one at a time, each with a reusable derivative theorem,
   interval enclosure, and computed domain-certificate rule; and
6. measure complete real leaves, including enclosure production and theorem
   handoff, while sharing AST reification and operator setup across boxes.

This route spends more proof effort up front than proving the cubic contract
directly, but it is the route that can amortize over the full nonlinear
workload.

### First universal proof slice completed

Commit `de84a91` implements and verifies the first slice of that route. It
defines an in-logic polynomial AST with exact rational constants, indexed
variables, negation, addition, multiplication, and square. For **every** AST
value, a structural theorem proves that the six components of the real jet
evaluator are exactly its symbolic value, first derivatives, and second
derivatives:

```text
candle_poly_real_jet_correct:
  !e x y.
    jet_f   (poly_real_jet x y e) = poly_value x y e /\
    jet_fx  (poly_real_jet x y e) = poly_dx    x y e /\
    jet_fy  (poly_real_jet x y e) = poly_dy    x y e /\
    jet_fxx (poly_real_jet x y e) = poly_dxx   x y e /\
    jet_fxy (poly_real_jet x y e) = poly_dxy   x y e /\
    jet_fyy (poly_real_jet x y e) = poly_dyy   x y e
```

A second structural theorem proves compiler execution for an arbitrary
initial stack, not just the empty program entry point:

```text
candle_poly_compile_run:
  !e x y stack.
    real_jet_run x y (poly_compile e) stack =
    CONS (poly_real_jet x y e) stack
```

Their combined corollary states that the compiled postfix program returns all
six symbolic components for every AST expression. All these theorems have
empty hypothesis lists. The cubic fixture compiles to the exact expected
12-instruction program, and a fresh full Candle replay ends with
`CANDLE_CV_POLYNOMIAL_EXPR_JET_OK`.

This closes the general algebraic evaluator/compiler correspondence for the
current two-variable polynomial language. It does **not** yet prove that
`poly_dx`, `poly_dxx`, and their siblings are the actual HOL/Flyspeck
`partial`/`partial2` derivatives. That calculus correspondence is the next
reusable theorem. Dimension-parametric jets, operator-domain predicates, and
non-polynomial constructors also remain open. No expression-specific calculus
proof was introduced.

Commit `2ec4cbf` makes the source-language validity hypothesis executable.
The current two-coordinate fragment accepts only variable indices 0 and 1.
Although the internal program evaluator remains total, source reification must
prove this validity predicate; it cannot reinterpret an out-of-range source
variable as the evaluator's zero default. The focused replay proves that the
cubic fixture is valid, rejects variable index 2, and again ends with
`CANDLE_CV_POLYNOMIAL_EXPR_JET_OK`.

The current expression family includes exact rational constants, two
variables, negation, addition, multiplication, and the reifier's exact square
instruction. Transcendentals, division, square root, domain guards, and
subdivision orchestration remain outside this bounded prototype.

For the production language, the intended final reusable theorem is stronger
than the intermediate contract and should make the scaling boundary explicit:

```text
WHOLE_BOX_CHECK_SOUND:
  valid_dim N e /\
  reifies source_function e /\
  valid_box box /\
  domain_certificate_ok e box /\
  whole_box_check e box certificate = Accept result
  ==>
    m_taylor_interval (denote e) box
      (center box) (radii box)
      (value_bounds result)
      (gradient_bounds result)
      (hessian_bounds result) /\
    (!x. x IN interval [box]
         ==> source_function x <= upper_bound result)
```

Here `domain_certificate_ok` is a computed, proved predicate covering the
side conditions of every partial operator (nonzero denominator, positive
square-root/logarithm argument, inverse-trigonometric range, and so on).
`reifies source_function e` is the mechanically generated connection to the
original Flyspeck term.  Neither is permission for a new hand-written calculus
proof.  The general library proof must show once that every accepted language
node has the claimed value, gradient, Hessian, and smoothness on its certified
domain; Flyspeck's existing Taylor theorem then supplies the remainder and
box-bound conclusion.

Thus the genuinely per-expression work is limited to checked data:

1. reification equality from the original term to its AST;
2. variable/dimension validity and a computed domain certificate; and
3. the particular box/checker acceptance result.

The still-general work is dimension-parametric jets, the Flyspeck
`partial`/`partial2` packaging, construction of `m_lin_approx` and
`second_bounded` from the computed enclosures, and one correctness rule for
each newly supported operator.  None of those obligations should scale with
the number of expressions using the operator.

## Generic checker-to-theorem handoff

The reflected numerical side now has a kernel-checked, expression-independent
handoff theorem.  In schematic form:

```text
JET_WHOLE_BOX_ACCEPT_SOUND:
  taylor_contract program box f /\
  whole_box_accept program box
  ==>
  (!x. x IN box ==> f x < 0)
```

The proof also exposes the non-strict upper-bound lemma separately.  It uses
the exact interval containment theorems for the computed value, gradient and
Hessian jet, proves monotonicity of the two-coordinate Taylor expression, and
then converts the reflected negative upper bound into the original real
inequality.  It does not construct intermediate numerical arithmetic
theorems per expression or per box.

This is the downstream half of the scalable architecture.  The upstream half
remains the universal language theorem described above: valid AST plus a
checked domain certificate must imply the Taylor contract for its denotation.
Once that is established, a source instance needs only mechanical reification,
box/domain data, and an accepted computed verdict.  It must not require a new
calculus proof merely because the expression is new.

## Plan consequence

The experiment supports continuing the architecture. The next bounded steps
are:

1. finish the reusable analytic/source-derivative bridge in the nonlinear
   support state;
2. produce the original theorem for this complete box from the accepted
   verdict;
3. replay one captured real leaf and compare complete bound production plus
   final theorem handoff against the legacy verifier;
4. replay the varied captured leaves without rebuilding shared expression
   setup; and
5. broaden the expression and domain-checking language only as those real
   leaves require.

The architectural bet remains conditional. A fast synthetic polynomial is
evidence that `Kernel.compute` can absorb the complete numerical boundary; it
is not yet evidence that transcendental enclosures, real Flyspeck expression
shapes, or subdivision-scale denominator growth will remain fast.
