## Result

Candle commit `9c3d35f4` is pushed on
`codex/cv-nonlinear-disjunctive-v1`.

The universal compact soundness theorem contains `dimindex(:N)`. The previous
adapter first substituted the large concrete token stream, decoded job batch,
and root box, then rewrote `dimindex(:6)`. That final rewrite traversed a
theorem already expanded with all concrete certificate data.

The adapter now:

1. specializes only the source expression and six-dimensional type witness;
2. rewrites the dimension while tokens, jobs, and root remain variables; and
3. only then specializes the concrete token stream, jobs, and root.

This is a theorem-construction ordering change only. It does not alter the
universal theorem, result decoder, computed verdict, source theorem, or
accepted axioms.

## Discriminating evidence

A focused eight-cell probe separately specialized the source, type witness,
tokens, jobs, and root. Those five substitutions took about 4.1 seconds in
total. Rewriting the dimension afterward took **45.0 seconds** because the
rewrite traversed the fully concrete theorem. The diagnostic proof had
already emitted its success marker, but its restored runtime then exited
while retaining every large intermediate theorem; it is therefore retained
only as discriminating development evidence, not as a checkpoint result.

The production change was then tested independently from clean predecessor
checkpoints on both genuine fixtures.

## Remaining scaling costs

The optimization removes the identified late-rewrite multiplier, but doubling
the batch from four to eight still increases the post-numerical path from
7.464 to 24.474 seconds. The largest eight-cell proof-side phases are now:

- analytic soundness composition: 13.279 seconds, including 6.215 seconds in
  `MATCH_MP` after specialization;
- complete result decoding: 6.503 seconds; and
- correspondence: 2.582 seconds.

These phases still traverse terms proportional to the concrete batch. The
next optimization should target one of them with a focused discriminator; it
should not reintroduce per-leaf theorem construction or weaken the single
universal soundness boundary.
