## Outcome

The fixed-scale signed word interval multiplication used by the direct-`cake`
historical checker now has a complete mathematical containment theorem.  For
valid input intervals containing integer numerators `left_value` and
`right_value`, every successful unchecked product encloses their exact product

same guarantee without requiring validity as a caller-supplied premise:
successful return itself implies that both inputs passed the fail-closed
validity check.

The new proof layer includes:

- multiplication monotonicity for nonnegative and nonpositive integer
  multipliers;
- exact endpoint-product bounds for all nine combinations of nonnegative,
  nonpositive, and mixed input intervals;
- a mathematical containment predicate for represented integer numerators;
- lifting lemmas for the two-corner and four-corner interval constructors;
- `bounded_interval64_mul_unchecked_contains_product`; and
- `bounded_interval64_mul_contains_product` for the public checked boundary.

The mixed/mixed case follows the implementation's minimum/maximum result: its
lower and upper witnesses are expressed as the appropriate disjunctions over
the four corner products, rather than assuming one fixed corner is extremal.
Directed floor and ceiling rounding, overflow rejection, sign dispatch, and
the checked validity boundary are all therefore covered by one reusable
theorem chain.
