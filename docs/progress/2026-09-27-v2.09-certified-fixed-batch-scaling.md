## Fixed-hybrid one-verdict batches

Candle commit `d43b4285` adds the logical batch contract, executable batch
representation theorem, general batch soundness theorem, and proof-producing
adapter for the fixed-polynomial hybrid.  Each batch performs one aggregate
`Kernel.compute`; its returned bit is connected to a theorem that every exact
job in the batch satisfies the universal certified checker.

The genuine action-296 scaling test used exact distinct subdivisions of an
accepted certificate cell, with source/program preparation shared once.  All
three batches produced assumption-free acceptance theorems:

| Batch | Rational compute | Fixed compute | Compute speedup | Rational total | Fixed total | Total speedup |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 boxes | 17.6062 s | 1.9261 s | 9.14x | 18.7727 s | 3.1381 s | 5.98x |
| 64 boxes | 161.3204 s | 63.0574 s | 2.56x | 180.7978 s | 83.9137 s | 2.15x |

The one-box interval was below the external observer's useful timing
resolution, so it is not used for a speedup claim.  In total, **73/73** real
box checks were accepted across the 1, 8, and 64 batches.  Peak sampled RSS
was unchanged at 2,253,704 KiB.

For the 64-box fixed run, the measured batch boundary separates into:

- cell and job encoding before evaluator entry: approximately **9.65 s**;
- aggregate `Kernel.compute`: **63.06 s**;
- acceptance reconstruction, erasure checking, and theorem assembly after
  evaluator return: approximately **11.21 s**.

The corresponding rational pre/post costs were approximately 8.90 s and
10.58 s.  Thus the roughly 20-second proof/encoding cost is independent of the
arithmetic backend and is now the next reusable overhead to examine.  The
fixed numerical evaluator still dominates at 64 boxes.

Cold preparation remains visible.  In the fixed run:

- shared whole-box source preparation: 5.1540 s;
- shared point-plan compilation: 1.2970 s;
- 64 per-box variants: 50.2071 s.

Charging the shared preparation, 64 variants, and the theorem-producing batch
once gives approximately 140.57 s fixed versus 233.22 s rational, a **1.66x**
cold end-to-end improvement for this sample.

## Interpretation and next boundary

The performance note's rational-normalization diagnosis now generalizes from
two cells to a theorem-producing 64-box batch.  The speedup declines from the
8-box to the 64-box sample, so fixed arithmetic has not removed all numerical
scaling costs.  The next useful work is therefore:

1. profile the 9.65-second encoding and 11.21-second theorem-handoff regions
   more finely and cache only data/theorems that are genuinely shared;
2. sample several distinct pass leaves from the complete 1,061-leaf
   action-296 certificate to measure additional subdivision demand and
   expression/box variability; and
3. use that sample to choose between optimizing remaining rational nonlinear
   operations, box preparation, or batch theorem assembly before attempting
   the complete certificate.

This keeps the next experiment tied to end-to-end nonlinear verification
rather than isolated arithmetic microbenchmarks.
