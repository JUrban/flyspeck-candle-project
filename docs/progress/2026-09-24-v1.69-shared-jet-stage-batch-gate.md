## Bounded lower-level batching gate

The matched v1.68 comparison showed that the exact shared-jet formal proof is
currently 1.37x--1.73x slower than legacy on the genuine 16-leaf certificate.
Before building any new batch soundness layer, a disposable focused probe
tested the cheapest relevant prerequisite:

- collect the exact 16 live leaf domains from the genuine adaptive precision
  tree;
- normalize and encode their six-coordinate boxes exactly as the passing
  scalar adapter does;
- retain the one prepared 87-instruction source program;
- replace the scalar path's 96 `Kernel.compute` calls with six calls, each
  receiving a nested pair of the 16 jobs for one existing computation stage;
  and
- project each returned equality back to its individual job and require the
  same accepted result as the scalar computation.

This stays below the previously rejected high-level monolithic checker. The
nested-pair term was accepted by `Kernel.compute`, so this was a real evaluator
measurement rather than a translation failure.

## Stop result

The run emitted the authenticated fresh-input acknowledgement and

```text
CANDLE_CV_REAL_STAGE_BATCH_BEGIN mode=batch
```

then remained CPU-active inside the six-call batch block. It had not reached
the batch-end marker after more than 150 seconds. By comparison:

- the complete current reflected formal proof, including soundness and leaf
  theorem construction, takes approximately 24 seconds;
- the scalar numerical compute subphases aggregate to roughly 9--10 seconds
  over all 16 leaves; and
- the complete matched reflected proof plus final assembly takes under 30
  seconds.

The disposable batch was therefore terminated at the bounded gate. Its
detached DMTCP child was identified and stopped explicitly; the protected
cumulative verifier was not touched and advanced during the experiment.

No batch result, analytic theorem, certificate theorem, action credit, S2/S3
evidence, qualification, promotion, or release claim is made.

## Decision

No batch implementation is retained. Building list correctness, analytic
soundness, and live-domain transport around a numerical boundary already more
than an order of magnitude slower than scalar evaluation would not advance the
project.

This independently agrees with the older reflected result-list measurements:
large nested result theorems have superlinear representation and reconstruction
cost in the current evaluator. Reducing the number of `Kernel.compute` calls
does not compensate for returning one large theorem tree.

The passing scalar shared-jet implementation remains authoritative. Smart
symbolic differentiation and lower-level result batching both remain
deferred. The next nonlinear work should instead use real source coverage:

1. inventory the action-296 source's square-root and `dihatn_x` expansion
   without running its 1,061-leaf certificate;
2. identify the smallest general reflected-language extension needed by that
   authenticated expression; and
3. retain the existing genuine certificate and legacy path as exact theorem
   oracles while extending the universal analytic theorem constructor by
   constructor.
