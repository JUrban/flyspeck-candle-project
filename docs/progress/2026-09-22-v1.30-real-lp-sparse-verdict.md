## Interpretation

The experiment confirms the proposed architecture and isolates the remaining
problem. In v11, source/number conversion consumes 77% of sparse terminal time;
`Kernel.compute` consumes 12%, proof preparation 8%, and final handoff less
than 1%.

The selector cache gives only a moderate benefit because the dominant residual
is repeated traversal of the full 424-variable basis while constructing each
row-denotation equality, not the cost of proving an individual `EL` selection.
More cache layers are therefore unlikely to close the remaining 4.14x gap.

The next LP prototype should construct each sparse row-correspondence theorem
structurally from:

1. the source `lin_f` entries;
2. the once-proved basis selector theorems; and
3. sparse-vector cons/addition congruence;

without applying a depth conversion across the whole basis per row. Immutable
row preparation should then be tested for reuse across terminals and, where
the same inequality rows recur, across certificates. The complete-verdict
evaluator and generic soundness theorem should be retained unchanged.

loading required sources through `verify_all.hl`, with action 170 deliberately
omitted. Its SHA-256 is
`7047c93688c0f49b4c16b55f6fb609bdfb6e7fdf92ca1bda8d370297d8f808a5`.
It reduced each real-terminal experiment to minutes and did not affect the
protected cumulative worker.

The late state exposed four source-stability issues in the new prototype:

- untyped theorem binder `index` captured Flyspeck's `index` constant;
- the denotation normalizer needed proof-producing `EL_CONV` for large
  numeral selections;
- lambda binder `row` captured Flyspeck's polymorphic `row` constant; and
- global/module `int_of_num` bindings were shadowed by HOL/Flyspeck code.

All were repaired narrowly without changing certificate arithmetic or theorem
interfaces. The final selector cache is keyed directly by HOL numerals, so it
does not require a shadow-prone host-integer conversion.
