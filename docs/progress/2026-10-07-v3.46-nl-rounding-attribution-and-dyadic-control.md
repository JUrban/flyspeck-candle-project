## Bounded lazy-completion candidate

A first candidate fuses each maximal consecutive outer-add run. It sums the
already completed center jets and Hessians exactly and performs the Taylor
completion once at the end of the run. This preserves the derivative/Taylor
formula and fixed representation while removing intermediate results that no
subsequent non-add operation observes.

All 4,173 final bounds are byte-identical to the unfused lane and all
certificates remain accepted. Twenty warmed, order-balanced pairs gave:

| Lane | Preparation | Evaluation | Complete batch | Evaluation quotients |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Current decimal fixed graph | 0.335920806 s | 0.357365047 s | 0.693285854 s | 5,691,972 |
| Fused consecutive additions | 0.333430569 s | 0.324128037 s | 0.657558606 s | 4,990,908 |

The candidate removes 701,064 quotients (12.32%), improves recurring
evaluation by 1.103 times, and improves the cold complete batch by only 1.054
times. It is useful general lazy-completion machinery, but is not the large
architecture by itself. Evidence is in
`nl-native-case10173-fused-add-paired-v3-dev-001`; the phase-table SHA-256 is
`d9a178b5ba4c06ed90523bacba0febdb17ca5496ea4d28fa11c122d70391851b`.

## Arithmetic-only dyadic control

The second discriminator keeps the generic source instruction graph, dense
derivative/Taylor representation, signed-128 endpoints, and every rounding
boundary fixed. Both lanes use scale `2^23`, close to the established decimal
`10^7` scale. The candidate replaces exact integer division with signed exact
floor/ceiling shifts only when the denominator is the scale, scale squared, or
twice scale squared. All other denominators retain exact division. Signed edge
cases, including minimum and maximum 128-bit values, are cross-checked against
division at startup.

The shift and division lanes produce byte-identical bounds and both accept all
4,173 certificates. Twenty warmed, order-balanced pairs gave:

## Interpretation and next gate

The original controlled decomposition remains important: roughly 15 times
from exact-output-matched native equations to encoded Candle, and roughly 341
times from the original generic native fixed-scale equations to historical
specialized C++. Successive native changes have already reduced the latter on
this fixture; the dyadic-shift candidate is 3.542 times the historical
batch. That is progress in an unproved native prototype, not yet a reduction
of the end-to-end formally checked cost, and it does not erase the original
evidence about where the opportunity came from.

The two proof targets remain bounded:

1. consecutive-add lazy completion is a small general lemma and should reuse
   the existing lazy-completion machinery rather than create a case-specific
   theorem;
2. dyadic directed shift rounding needs one general signed quotient theorem
   plus a range/scale argument, but only after the composed full-batch gate.

The next larger-gap discriminator holds the winning arithmetic fixed and
changes the numerical graph. A first spot check of the historical
derivative/dihedral graph on the dyadic-shift backend remained slower (about
0.335 seconds), so that already rejected graph will not receive proof work.
The next native alternatives should instead reduce complete derivative/Taylor
work per box: begin with a compact specialized square-root/dihedral primitive
or a complete low-work box instruction derived from the historical C++
routine, and measure full acceptance, bound tightness, preparation, recurring
execution, and memory. Only a material complete-batch win advances to a
general instruction theorem and a fresh Candle/native measurement.
