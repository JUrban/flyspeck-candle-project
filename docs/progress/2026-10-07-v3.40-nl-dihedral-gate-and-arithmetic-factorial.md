## Why this packet ran

The previous fixed-native work left two distinct gaps:

1. encoded Candle versus native fixed execution; and
2. native fixed execution versus the historical specialized C++ verifier.

The purpose of this packet was to keep those gaps separate.  It compared the
historical and fixed implementations at a matched dihedral boundary, applied
the complete 4,173-box acceptance/time gate to the candidate specialization,
and then changed arithmetic while holding the accepted algorithm fixed.

## A boundary comparison caught a false identity

Both native harnesses now emit diagnostic-only angle value, center-gradient,
and Hessian data on the same genuine boxes.  The first comparison exposed that
the rejected partial specialization used the false identity

`4*x0*delta = U126*U135`.

The correct relation is

`U126*U135 = 4*x0*delta + delta_x4^2`.

The bad candidate's center-gradient intervals did not always overlap the
historical values.  Its earlier 89/128 result is withdrawn as evidence.  It
was already development-only and rejected, and no proof work depended on it.
This is a useful validation result: the matched primitive boundary prevented
formal work on an invalid numerical plan.

## Complete specialization gate: rejected

The full experiment derived a 4,173-job development fixture from the captured
boxes, computed tight square-root enclosures internally, and ran 20
order-balanced pairs.  Both lanes accepted every box:

| Lane | Mean preparation | Mean evaluation | Mean batch | Accepted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Generic fixed derivative path | 0.191086213 s | 0.939053166 s | 1.130139379 s | 4,173/4,173 |
| Corrected dihedral specialization | 0.188424105 s | 0.950126066 s | 1.138550171 s | 4,173/4,173 |

The specialization was 1.18% slower in evaluation and 0.74% slower for the
complete batch.  More importantly, it was wider on 3,863 boxes and tighter on
only 310.  The minimum margin below zero fell from `8.5845e-5` to
`4.00273e-5`.

This fails the integration gate.  The present specialization will receive no
analytic-containment or authenticated-dispatch proof.  The reusable lesson is
that precise certificates and optimized range primitives can recover
historical angle bounds; composing those formulas in this fixed evaluator
does not improve complete-certificate throughput or final bounds.

## Decision and next large-gap experiment

The current specialization is closed as a numerical dead end.  No proof work
is authorized by these results.  The general integer-square-root enclosure,
source classification, and matched-boundary diagnostics remain reusable, but
they should advance to formalization only as part of a measured winning plan.

The next native experiment should hold the accepted operation graph and
4,173-box fixture fixed while changing endpoint execution more radically:

1. replace binary-search integer square root with either externally prepared
   certificates plus cheap squaring checks, or a hardware-seeded corrected
   integer square root;
2. measure that change separately from a hardware-rounded interval endpoint
   lane;
3. require 4,173/4,173 acceptance and charge preparation once;
4. compare against the 0.407803029-second accepted `10^7` lane and the
   0.082490265-second historical specialized lane;
5. only then select reusable rounding, square-root, or sparse-jet theorems.

The experiment now assigns real portions of the gap: checked arithmetic is
substantial, numeric magnitude is substantial, and an approximately fivefold
native gap remains after both are reduced.  That residual—not the rejected
dihedral proof—is the next architectural target.  The much larger original
encoded-Candle/native and native/historical gaps remain separate metrics; no
orders-of-magnitude end-to-end speedup is claimed here.
