## Assessment

The source observations in
`docs/advice/Candle_NL_Performance_Research_2026-09-25.md` were useful, but
the later measured work has narrowed their interpretation substantially.
They do not point to one remaining setup bug.  Instead, they helped separate
three concerns: work already removed, plausible-looking source costs that
measured small or negative, and the genuinely material numerical and
preparation costs.

### Already covered

- The universal centered-Taylor soundness theorem, source reifier,
  certificate erasure, fixed-polynomial invariant, fixed batch theorem, exact
  split-tree cover, and compact root handoff are now proved.  The genuine
  action-296 parent theorem is assumption-free and retains MD5
  `926f44ab8d5f2303064fe5788cead588`.
- One internal batch performs one `Kernel.compute` and returns one aggregate
  verdict.  Empty/constant controls put fixed evaluator setup at about 40 ms
  or below the 0.05-second observer resolution, rather than near the
  64-box numerical cost.
- The discarded `REAL_RAT_REDUCE_CONV` work in untrusted square-root hint
  preparation was replaced by exact ordinary `Num` evaluation, with the old
  conversion retained as an oracle.  Compiling the seven point expressions
  once per source then reduced 64-box preparation by about 63% relative to
  the already repaired path.
- Full diagnostic Taylor results no longer cross the production theorem
  boundary per leaf.  The fixed batch and exact tree adapter consume a compact
  verdict and perform one source-level handoff.

### Measured secondary or negative candidates

- Replacing the 128-pair GCD fuel with a numeric countdown changed a
  512-call test from 0.0069 to 0.0055 seconds.  Fuel construction does not
  explain a minute-scale batch, although normalization of large rationals did
  remain relevant.
- Constant and variable completion cost about 6 ms per six-dimensional
  result.  Shortcuts may be tidy, but are not a primary explanation.
- Explicit sharing of the four interval endpoint products was about 8x
  slower in the focused test.
- A proved Hessian-only multiplication projection had no stable throughput
  benefit on repeated genuine 1/8/64-box runs and was removed.
- A generic instrumented ordinary interpreter was 2.59--2.63x slower than
  Candle's fast evaluator.  Merely moving the same interpreter outside
  `Kernel.compute` is therefore not a useful optimization.

### Costs that remain material

General rational normalization was a real cost, not merely a source-level
possibility.  The proved fixed-scale polynomial hybrid reduced the genuine
64-box `Kernel.compute` phase from 161.3204 to 63.0574 seconds (**2.56x**) and
the complete theorem-producing batch from 180.7978 to 83.9137 seconds
(**2.15x**).  At eight boxes the compute speedup was **9.14x**.  The falling
speedup at 64 boxes shows that the current hybrid still has a substantial
scaling cost.  Analytic inverse, square root, arctangent, conversions back to
the rational Taylor representation, dense list/pair traversal, and evaluator
allocation remain plausible components; the existing result does not yet
separate them.

Cold preparation and theorem-boundary work also remain visible.  In the
fixed 64-box run, per-box variant preparation took about 50.21 seconds.
Inside the theorem-producing batch boundary, cell/job encoding took about
9.65 seconds and acceptance reconstruction, erasure checks, and theorem
assembly took about 11.21 seconds.  These costs do not explain the numerical
kernel by themselves, but they materially reduce amortized end-to-end gains.
The likely durable remedy is to keep one authenticated compiled expression
and supply changing certificate intervals and boxes as checked data, rather
than compile a new expression variant for every cell.

## Revised experimental priority

1. Measure acceptance and required extra subdivision on small groups of real
   leaves.  Compare per-leaf box certificates with local envelopes of 2, 4,
   and 8 adjacent leaves, and sample more than one region of the precision
   tree.  Charge shared preparation once per group.
2. Profile the fixed hybrid by authentic top-level instruction on a small set
   of accepted final cells.  This should distinguish fixed polynomial work
   from remaining rational inverse/square-root/arctangent and completion work
   without adding another general framework.
3. Split the measured per-box preparation into certificate-data generation,
   variant theorem construction, program encoding, and job construction.
   Only then prototype a shared-source/data-only certificate interface.
4. Defer more GCD-fuel, endpoint-sharing, constant-node, or Hessian-projection
   rewrites unless new measurements contradict the focused negative results.

This ordering preserves the completed general soundness proof.  It first
tests whether the architecture keeps the existing certificate tree compact,
then optimizes the recurring numerical path that remains after fixed-scale
polynomial execution.
