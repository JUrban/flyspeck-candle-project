## Bounded question

The complete case-16594 control had already separated two costs while holding
the generic 167-instruction derivative/Taylor graph fixed:

- MPZ versus checked signed 256-bit endpoints changed representation while
  preserving decimal scale, exact directed rounding, and all 875 results;
- checked 256-bit versus padded hardware endpoints left a further 6.6-times
  recurring gap, but changed both representation and quotient rounding.

This packet holds the checked 256-bit representation and generic graph fixed,
then changes only the fixed grid from decimal `10^12` with integer division to
dyadic `2^40` with exact signed shift rounding.  It also audits whether that
bounded arithmetic can be moved directly into the existing theorem-producing
`Kernel.compute` engine.

All 20,620,250 directed quotients in the dyadic evaluation use the exact shift
path; none falls back to division.  Decimal-to-dyadic improves recurring
evaluation by only **1.053 times**.  Checked dyadic arithmetic remains **6.252
times** slower than padded double.  Thus general integer division and the
choice of decimal grid account for only a small part of this second-family
gap; fixed-width multi-limb arithmetic and its pervasive checked operations
remain the larger representation cost.

Changing grids does not preserve exact output numerals, so the relevant gate
is enclosure and certificate acceptance.  The dyadic lane is tighter than the
decimal lane on 517 cells and wider on 358.  Its worst widening is about
`1.83e-7`, and its minimum accepted margin remains about `0.00109036`.  Padded
double is wider than dyadic on 828 cells and tighter on 47, but also accepts
all 875.  These finite comparisons do not replace a general outward-rounding
proof.

The earlier MPZ result, combined with this matched run, now separates the
arithmetic factors on one complete real certificate:

- MPZ to checked decimal: about 3.6 times, with byte-identical bounds;
- checked decimal to checked dyadic: 1.05 times, changing the exact grid and
  quotient implementation; and
- checked dyadic to padded double: 6.25 times, leaving the graph fixed but
  changing the scalar representation and rounding implementation.

## `Kernel.compute` boundary audit

The current verified computation boundary cannot directly realize the native
checked-width result:

- Candle head `011a92ea5447a40caec138b598f405e939d27405` defines `cval` as only
  `Cexp_num num | Cexp_pair cval cval` in `candle/compute.ml`;
- `COMPUTE_INIT_THMS` contains exactly the 62 natural-number and pair
  equations required by the kernel, and reordered or shortened contracts fail
  closed;
- CakeML head `dcc03f2866f05b1db18b9f45c731ce45c3a3133e` implements the fast
  verified executor with `cv = Num num | Pair cv cv`; its add, multiply,
  divide, and remainder operations are over unbounded naturals; and
- the separate compiled Candle runtime exposes verified `Cake.Word64` and
  `Cake.Double` operations, but those values are not in the
  theorem-producing compute language.

Adding words to `Kernel.compute` would therefore mean extending its expression
and value datatypes, characteristic-equation contract, fast executor, HOL
term translation, and correctness proof, followed by a new verified Candle
runtime build.  Calling the existing compiled `Word64` operations from an
ordinary checker would be fast, but its returned boolean would not by itself
be a kernel theorem.  Neither is a bounded local arithmetic patch.

The earlier two-limb experiment also found that emulating words from paired
small naturals inside the existing compute language was 1.06--1.09 times
slower even before charging signed intervals and overflow checks.  That route
remains rejected.

## Bounded specialization and larger-gap plan

The current exact-angle proof remains bounded.  It may finish if the remaining
proof is small or supplies reusable source-correspondence machinery, but it
will not expand into more case-specific calculus solely on the present local
speedup.

The next cheap native discriminator will use the six repeated case-16594
polynomial blocks rather than another formula substitution:

1. authenticate one 39/85-step pair and prepare its instruction sequence once;
2. execute the exact same operation order, rounding locations, Taylor
   completion, and box data through a prepared straight-line path;
3. compare that path with the current decoded stack executor first under
   padded double and then under checked dyadic arithmetic;
4. require identical outputs for each fixed arithmetic lane and 875/875
   complete-certificate acceptance; and
5. charge preparation once, recurring execution, memory, and the full batch.

This holds arithmetic fixed while changing dispatch and intermediate
materialization.  It deliberately differs from the rejected fused-polynomial
probe, which changed enclosure boundaries and lost 17 cells.  A material win
would identify a general compiled-instruction theorem worth proving.  A small
win stops this packet and assigns the cost to scalar exact arithmetic rather
than generic derivative dispatch.

The broader programme remains two-lane:

1. **Numerical algorithm lane.** Continue faithful complete-box comparisons
   with the historical specialized verifier and real certificate families.
   Port algorithms, derivative tables, and enclosure decisions in native code
   first, then cross the winning data flow to exact outward arithmetic.  The
   historical approximately 340-times figure remains a starting-architecture
   comparison, not the residual optimized case-10173 gap.
2. **Verified execution lane.** Keep the current natural-number reflected
   checker as the proof-safe baseline.  If several real families confirm a
   large checked-width complete-batch win after algorithm improvements, scope
   a separate CakeML project to add overflow-returning bounded words to the
   verified compute engine.  Its gate is end-to-end theorem time and explicit
   exact fallback, not native microbenchmarks.  Until that evidence exists,
   the kernel extension is deferred.

For both lanes, complete-certificate acceptance, bound quality, preparation
at its actual reuse scope, theorem handoff, peak memory, and total elapsed time
remain the decision measures.  Native results guide the architecture but
never serve as proof or as a release oracle.
