## Question and controls

The complete case-16594 arithmetic factorial left a 6.25-times gap between
checked 256-bit dyadic arithmetic and padded hardware endpoints on the same
167-instruction derivative/Taylor graph.  Its instruction profile placed
about 2.60 seconds of checked execution in three repeated 39/85-step
polynomial pairs.  This packet tests two explanations without changing the
mathematical graph:

1. whether decoding stack instructions and materializing their operands on
   every box is a significant part of the polynomial cost; and
2. what widths the exact stored values and arithmetic intermediates actually
   require on two complete real certificate families.

The prepared-executor control authenticates one 39/85-step pair, resolves its
constants, variables, and opcodes once, and then performs the exact same
fixed-scale operations in the same order.  It neither fuses instructions nor
moves a rounding boundary.  The width control instruments the unchanged
checked-256 implementation.  Both controls require full certificate
acceptance.

## Prepared execution is a negative result

Seven rotated repetitions on all 875 case-16594 cells give:

| Lane | Mean preparation | Mean evaluation | Mean batch | Accepted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Checked dyadic baseline | 0.043395 s | **4.952556 s** | 4.995951 s | 875/875 |
| Checked dyadic prepared pair | 0.050059 s | **4.963015 s** | 5.013075 s | 875/875 |
| Padded double baseline | 0.047291 s | **0.768459 s** | 0.815750 s | 875/875 |
| Padded double prepared pair | 0.041789 s | **0.779320 s** | 0.821109 s | 875/875 |

Within each arithmetic backend, the baseline and prepared result records are
byte-identical.  The prepared path is nevertheless slightly slower in both
balanced batches: 0.998-times the baseline speed under checked arithmetic and
0.986-times under padded double.  Peak memory is unchanged within 8 KiB.

The focused profile agrees.  Under checked arithmetic the selected 39-step
block takes 254.48 ms before and 254.75 ms after preparation; the 85-step
block takes 605.82 ms before and 606.27 ms after.  Padded double saves about
4.6 ms locally across the pair, but this does not survive as a complete-batch
win.  Repeated decode/dispatch and operand lookup are therefore not a
material explanation of the checked/hardware gap.  No instruction-specific
soundness proof is justified by this result, and the specialization stops
here.

## Complete-family width measurements

The range profiler was run with checked signed 256-bit arithmetic on the full
875-cell case-16594 certificate and the full accepted 4,173-cell optimized
case-10173 dihedral family.  Decimal and dyadic grids both pass every cell.

| Family and grid | Stored/result max | Product max | Addition max | Quotient numerator max | Quotient denominator max |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| case16594 decimal `10^12` | 64 bits | 104 bits | 92 bits | **135 bits** | 81 bits |
| case16594 dyadic `2^40` | 64 bits | 104 bits | 92 bits | **135 bits** | 82 bits |
| case10173 decimal `10^10` | 55 bits | 88 bits | 65 bits | 114 bits | 68 bits |
| case10173 dyadic `2^23` | 45 bits | 68 bits | 44 bits | 83 bits | 48 bits |

The input conversion maxima are 47 bits for case16594 and 36/26 bits for the
decimal/dyadic case10173 lanes.  Thus the earlier case-16594 `int128` failure
is not caused by wide persistent endpoints: scale-squared Taylor
recomposition creates a 135-bit quotient numerator while the rounded values
remain at most 64 bits.  Merely substituting 64- or 128-bit storage for the
whole evaluator cannot be sound.  A plausible next native design instead
uses narrow stored endpoints with a checked wider product/quotient
accumulator and an explicit overflow result or exact fallback.

These observations are finite workload evidence, not a universal range
proof.  In particular, they do not by themselves authorize a 192-bit bound
for arbitrary Flyspeck expressions.  A production reflected checker must
either validate a prepared-program range contract or make every bounded
operation total by returning overflow and falling back to the existing exact
checker.

## What the two historical gaps now mean

The two performance boundaries remain separate, but their scopes must be
stated precisely:

- the roughly 340-times number was the **starting** generic native
  fixed-scale versus historical specialized-C++ comparison on case10173;
  the controlled native algorithm work has already reduced that fixture's
  residual optimized-native gap to about 1.35 times; and
- the still-large verified-execution boundary is about 12--34 times for
  matched derivative computations inside Candle, while the independent
  case-16594 arithmetic control leaves a 6.25-times checked-256/hardware gap
  on the same native graph.

Consequently the broader investigation remains active, but another
source-level polynomial specialization is not the next step.  The cheapest
discriminator is a checked narrower accumulator on the exact same graphs and
boxes.  It will hold the derivative algorithm and rounding sequence fixed,
require exact output equality and complete acceptance, and report preparation,
evaluation, peak memory, and overflow.  Only a substantial recurring win
across both real families can justify scoping a verified bounded-word compute
extension.

In parallel, the proof-safe architecture continues to target a flatter
natural-number `Kernel.compute` batch.  Native bounded-word results guide
that decision but never serve as proof or as a release oracle.
