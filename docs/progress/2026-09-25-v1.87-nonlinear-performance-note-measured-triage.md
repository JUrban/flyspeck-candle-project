## Scope

The project-owner research note
`docs/advice/Candle_NL_Performance_Research_2026-09-25.md` correctly labels
its findings as source observations rather than measured causes. This report
tests three of the cheapest discriminators on the current reflected checker:

1. whether the 128-cell reflected GCD fuel is rebuilt at material cost;
2. how much the full six-dimensional completion path costs for constant and
   variable leaves on the genuine action-296 box; and
3. whether spelling the four interval endpoint products once, behind a helper,
   is faster than the current duplicated-looking minimum/maximum expression.

All experiments are DEVELOPMENT / NON-RELEASE. They use the sealed
action-296 Taylor-support checkpoint and fresh disposable restores. No
qualified runtime, source, report, transcript, or semantic-evidence byte was
changed.

## What the current architecture already covers

Several concerns in the note have already been removed or bounded:

- **Repeated evaluator setup.** The v1.86 empty-batch controls, using the same
  equation set as the real checker, were below the external observer's 0.05
  second resolution. The 64 distinct-box numerical phase was 154--156 seconds,
  so fixed `Kernel.compute` setup is not the recurring gap.
- **Repeated per-leaf evaluator calls.** The batch checker submits all jobs
  behind one reflected Boolean verdict and uses the general batch soundness
  theorem to recover mathematical acceptance. It does not reconstruct one
  large numerical theorem per leaf.
- **Proof-producing untrusted square-root preparation.** v1.86 replaced
  discarded `REAL_RAT_REDUCE_CONV` work with an exact untrusted `Num`
  evaluator while retaining the old conversion as an oracle. Genuine
  64-box preparation improved 2.66--2.69 times; preparation plus checked batch
  improved 1.72--1.74 times.
- **Reusable analytic proof.** The universal centered-Taylor theorem, source
  reifier, certificate erasure link, exact split-tree cover, and compact root
  handoff are already proved. The latest action-296 root integration retained
  theorem digest `926f44ab8d5f2303064fe5788cead588` and the unchanged axiom
  set.
- **Shared expression preparation.** The 1/8/64-box fixture prepares the
  authenticated source and whole-box square-root certificates once, then
  supplies distinct exact boxes and certificate-only center variants.

These facts do not prove that all setup and proof-linking costs are optimal.
They do show that they are not explanations for the measured 154--156 second
64-box numerical kernel.

## Experiment 1: pair-valued GCD fuel is not material

The production GCD chunk carries 128 steps as a nested `cval` pair spine. A
candidate kept the identical Euclidean recurrence but carried the countdown
as numeric `Cexp_num` data. The benchmark evaluated 512 copies of consecutive
Fibonacci inputs requiring 92 Euclidean remainder steps, then compared the
complete exact results.

| Fuel representation | 512-call wall time |
|---|---:|
| Current 128-cell pair fuel | 0.0069 s |
| Numeric reflected fuel | 0.0055 s |

The exact outputs matched. Both measurements are below 0.01 seconds and near
the timestamping granularity. This is strong evidence that `Kernel.compute`
compiles or shares the literal fuel rather than reconstructing its 128 pairs
for every normalization. Changing the fuel representation cannot explain, or
materially reduce, a 154--156 second batch.

This result is narrow. It does **not** show that rational normalization itself
is cheap on growing Taylor data. Large numerator/denominator arithmetic and
repeated normalization may still matter; the source-level fuel-constructor
count is simply not a useful proxy for that cost.

## Experiment 2: constant/variable completion is visible but small

The second fixture extracted the exact accepted action-296 probe box, computed
its center environment and rounded radii once, and then forced the complete
six-dimensional result structure through a compact structural checksum. Each
phase evaluated 64 results:

| Complete result constructor | 64-call wall time | Approximate per call |
|---|---:|---:|
| Polynomial constant | 0.383 s | 5.99 ms |
| Polynomial variable | 0.387 s | 6.05 ms |

Shared center/radius preparation was 0.635 seconds and was outside both timed
phases. The authentic source has 54 top-level analytic instructions. Charging
roughly 6 ms to all 54 instructions gives a useful reference scale of about
0.32 seconds per box, while the v1.86 64-box kernel averaged about 2.44 seconds
per box. This is not a formal decomposition: non-leaf operations perform much
more work, and their completion inputs are denser. It does show that
constant/variable-only shortcuts are unlikely to close the remaining gap.

Dense completion is still a plausible secondary target. A useful next test
would isolate `result_mul`, `result_inv`, and `result_sqrt` on genuine
intermediate jets rather than generalize from zero Hessians.

## Experiment 3: explicit endpoint sharing is slower

The reflected interval multiplication definition visibly repeats four exact
endpoint products below `min4` and `max4`. A candidate computed the products as
four strict helper arguments and reused those values for both extrema. The
benchmark forced complete interval results through a compact checksum and
also compared one complete exact result:

| Implementation | 1,024-call wall time |
|---|---:|
| Current duplicated-looking expression | 0.042 s |
| Explicit four-product helper | 0.341 s |

The exact outputs matched, but the helper version was about **8.0 times
slower**. The current evaluator is already sharing or simplifying the repeated
subexpressions effectively; adding a function boundary introduces overhead.
The proposed source rewrite is therefore rejected.

This is also a caution for source-only operation counts: syntactic duplication
does not necessarily imply repeated evaluator work.

## Revised assessment and next discriminator

The measurements change the priority of the note's suggestions:

- Do not redesign GCD fuel or add an endpoint-product helper.
- Do not prioritize specialized constant/variable completion. Its measured
  cost is real but too small to explain most of the remaining kernel time.
- Continue treating rational-size growth as relevant, but measure it on real
  intermediate Taylor values rather than infer it from the fuel syntax.
- The most credible unmeasured observation is the **full-jet-to-Hessian
  projection** in Taylor-model multiplication, inverse, square root, and
  arctangent. For example, `result_mul` computes a complete proxy jet and then
  retains only its Hessian, while its center value/gradient are computed by a
  separate first-jet path. A direct Hessian-only candidate can be checked for
  exact output equivalence on genuine intermediate data before any proof or
  production change.
- If that candidate is not materially faster, the next profiler should focus
  on arithmetic magnitude and normalization frequency along the actual
  54-instruction action-296 program, not add more general infrastructure.

The general soundness proof and genuine-leaf/root integration remain the main
architectural result. The probes here deliberately avoid redesigning them.
