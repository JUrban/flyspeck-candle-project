## Nonlinear exploration remains split into two tiers

The small post-Taylor Candle harness is already the edit loop for number
representation, whole interval/Taylor arithmetic, `Kernel.compute`, and
theorem handoff. Its existing real archived endpoint fixture completes in
14.23 seconds end to end, including a fresh restore, versus 155.46 seconds
from the earlier state. The theorem-producing calculation itself takes 0.58
CPU seconds. Every experiment uses a disposable fresh reflink copy and an
explicit success marker, so a partially loaded or failed process is never
continued.

The full nonlinear environment is restricted to work that genuinely requires
it. The active predecessor will stop before reconstruction, search, Taylor
construction, or proof. A second checkpoint will add and test the stable
proof-producing adapters once. A fresh capture from that state will then emit
the genuine first leaf's center, width, function bounds, derivative bounds,
Hessian bounds, and theorem conclusions as exact untrusted fixture data.

Those captured numbers can be replayed immediately in the small harness to
compare representation, source-plan construction, proof precomputation,
`Kernel.compute`, theorem handoff, and surrounding proof construction. Candle
will prove the result; the fixture will not be trusted as proof authority.
Only a coherent candidate that wins there will be rerun from a fresh full
nonlinear predecessor for integration confirmation.

This keeps expensive closure and adapter preparation reusable without turning
the experiment harness into a second verifier implementation.
