## Reflected sign-classification gate

The native sign-classified interval product cut endpoint products almost in
half and improved recurring native evaluation by 1.116 times. A bounded
`Kernel.compute` discriminator tested whether that operation reduction
survived the encoded evaluator before any proof or production integration.

The candidate implements the classical two-endpoint cases for all interval
sign combinations and delegates only the both-straddling case to the existing
four-endpoint definition. The benchmark cycles eight non-both-straddling sign
patterns through 4,096 calls. It ran eight order-balanced repetitions from the
stable fixed-core checkpoint and required every computed theorem to be closed
and every result to match exactly.

All **16/16** results were assumption-free and exactly equal. The external
observer measured repetitions 4 through 8 as follows:

| Lane | Fully charged five-call total | Mean | Median |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Existing four-endpoint product | 1.481961502 s | 0.296392300 s | 0.253846471 s |
| Sign-classified product | 1.660839838 s | 0.332167968 s | 0.245261897 s |

Most individually warm calls give the candidate only about a 3--5% advantage.
Allocation/GC spikes occur in both lanes, but the candidate encounters one
additional spike in this balanced sequence and is **1.121 times slower** in
the fully charged steady block. That cost is part of complete-checker
throughput and cannot be discarded merely because the median is slightly
better.

The candidate therefore fails the reflected materiality gate. No soundness
proof, production primitive change, or complete-checker integration will be
started. The benchmark is committed on the isolated Candle branch as
`d4c8fc8e` so the negative result remains reproducible.

This is consistent with the earlier canonical-product and endpoint-sharing
losses. Natural multiplication by zero is cheap in the evaluator, while cexp
sign dispatch, pair construction, and allocation can consume the native
operation-count saving.

## Arithmetic-width factorial on the prepared direct graph

The broader performance lane then held the prepared direct derivative/Taylor
graph fixed and changed its endpoint arithmetic:

- exact signed `int128` endpoints at scale `2^23`, with exact directed shifts;
- hardware `double` endpoints at the same scale, with every directed quotient
  padded outward by one fixed-scale unit.

Both lanes use sign-classified interval multiplication, the same source and
box preparation, coordinate-root formulas, dihedral tangent/Hessian graph,
Taylor completion, and all 4,173 genuine boxes. The `double` lane is an
untrusted containment diagnostic, not a proposed theorem backend.

Twenty warmed, order-balanced runs measured:

| Arithmetic lane | Preparation | Evaluation | Fully charged batch | Accepted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Exact `int128` | 0.409329380 s | 0.110726515 s | 0.520055896 s | 4,173 |
| Padded `double` | 0.359944562 s | **0.068002711 s** | **0.427947273 s** | 4,173 |

Changing arithmetic improves recurring evaluation by **1.628 times** and the
cold batch by **1.215 times**. Peak RSS falls from 41,764 to 38,280 KiB.

The gain is distributed rather than confined to one primitive:

| Stage | Exact `int128` | Padded `double` | Speedup |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Six coordinate roots | 0.013131588 s | 0.005952834 s | 2.206× |
| Dihedral tangent | 0.030906156 s | 0.018320371 s | 1.687× |
| Dihedral Hessian | 0.057215291 s | 0.037349531 s | 1.532× |
| Angle scale/addition | 0.004862846 s | 0.002726895 s | 1.783× |
| Final completion | 0.001939666 s | 0.001139619 s | 1.702× |

The rounded `double` path executes 0.68% more semantic interval products
because its wider intermediate values change a small amount of
rounding-dependent control. It nevertheless follows the same authenticated
source graph and accepts the complete batch.

## Bound quality

Padded `double` is wider on 4,173/4,173 boxes. Relative to exact `int128`, its
final upper bound increases by:

- minimum: `8.463859558105469e-06`;
- mean: `2.086525365599562e-05`;
- maximum: `4.267692565917969e-05`.

The minimum margin below zero falls from `4.971027374267578e-05` to
`3.826618194580078e-05`. Complete acceptance survives, but the loss confirms
that hardware timing cannot stand in for an exact formal result.

## What remains of the two large gaps

The original approximately **15-times** encoded-Candle/equivalent-native gap
and approximately **340-times** generic-native/specialized-C++ starting gap
remain distinct baselines for the current formal architecture.

The native direct-plan prototypes have removed much of the second gap on this
one fixture, but they are not yet a general proved checker. This factorial
shows that endpoint representation and rounding account for only 1.628 times
within that optimized graph. The reflected sign test also shows that deleting
individual products through existing cexp constructs will not close the
encoded-execution boundary.

Thus the orders-of-magnitude objective now depends on transferring a
low-work, reusable direct checker into the proof-producing path and avoiding
generic evaluator/dataflow overhead. It does not justify another isolated
scalar microkernel.

## Next concrete factorial and proof gate

The next cheap native experiment will hold the padded `double` backend, scale,
genuine boxes, and unprepared conversion boundary fixed while changing only
the derivative/Taylor data flow:

1. the best generic deferred-completion graph;
2. the direct source-authenticated graph, without its extra reusable-input
   preparation advantage.

It will measure the complete 4,173-box batch, stage and primitive counts,
bound tightness, acceptance, preparation, recurring execution, and memory.
This complements the arithmetic-width result without conflating the two
effects.

A material direct-graph winner must then pass a second expression/certificate
family. Only that cross-family result justifies a generally proved
complete-box instruction or equivalent universal checker extension. The proof
must authenticate source correspondence and preserve the universal analytic
bridge; it must not become a per-expression calculus proof. The resulting
instruction will then be remeasured inside `Kernel.compute`, where the
separate encoded-execution gap is decided.
