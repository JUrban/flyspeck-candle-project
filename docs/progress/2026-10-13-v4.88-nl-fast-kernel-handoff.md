## Concrete fast production route

The production design has two stages.

### Stage A: exact registered-result gate (now proved)

This is the smallest proof-producing integration test.  An offline theorem
authenticates one exact call/result pair; the Candle kernel then performs only
the structural dispatch and theorem construction on a hit.  It demonstrates
the theorem handoff without a roughly 200-second production replay, but it is
not suitable as a separate per-leaf registration scheme.

### Stage B: directly executed specialized checker (next)

The reusable production evaluator will:

1. authenticate the shared source/equation identity once per batch;
2. decode changing box, certificate, guard, and topology data into the fixed
   word representation;
3. execute the complete translated specialized checker;
4. encode its result in the wrapper's canonical `cval` form; and
5. use one general decoder/checker refinement theorem to satisfy
   `compute_fast_eval_ok` for all supported jobs.

`compute_with` and all downstream kernel soundness machinery can then be
reused unchanged.  The generic evaluator is the specification used in the
proof of the general refinement theorem; it is not rerun for each accepted
production call.

The existing specialized checker already covers the complete per-cell
center/box calculation, square-root and reciprocal certificates, domain and
geometry guards, outward-rounded source constant and coefficients, and the
Taylor verdict.  The smallest missing extension is therefore the canonical
authenticated decoder/result encoder plus its general refinement proof, not
another numerical checker layer.

## Remaining uncertainties

- The recurring cost of structural identity matching and theorem construction
  has not yet been measured in the compiled Candle kernel.
- Directly translating the full specialized checker into the kernel may add
  code-size or GC cost absent from its standalone executable.  The measured
  same-checker comparison therefore remains open.
- Embedding a fresh 271-equation value for every leaf would be too expensive.
  The batch design must share source identity and use compact per-job data.
- The general decoder/refinement theorem, rather than kernel handoff, is now
  the principal proof obligation for scalable fast execution.
- The live adapter from the accepted equality to the exact original Flyspeck
  inequality theorem remains to be exercised end to end.

The fast route is therefore no longer an unspecified future optimization:
its kernel theorem boundary is implemented and proved.  The acceptance
criterion for the next packet is actual compiled-kernel execution of this
genuine gate, followed by a second-cell/general-decoder result and a shared-
preparation batch measurement.
