- The residual expression-language problem is concentrated rather than an
  open-ended corpus-discovery problem.

## What this does not establish

- It does not show that every structurally expressible leaf obtains a negative
  Taylor verdict with the current enclosures or subdivision tree.
- It does not measure full-certificate runtime across the 7,517 presently
  expressible selected leaves.
- It does not replace the required connection from each computed certificate
  to its original Flyspeck theorem.
- It does not make a release claim.  The evidence remains development-only.

The practical remaining risk is therefore less about parsing 812 unrelated
formula shapes and more about numerical acceptance, efficient shared
preparation, complete tree replay, and compact theorem handoff.
