## Result

Candle commit `cb95e47f` adds a sequential mode to the generic adaptive-forest
proof adapter.  Each original Flyspeck root is now taken through source
preparation, point-plan compilation, final-cell construction, aggregate
reflected proof, source extraction, live-domain handoff, and recursive glue
before the next root begins.  Only the completed root theorem and its cell
count are retained.

This preserves the existing fail-closed checks for root order, exact source
and domain identity, split topology, final-cell count, theorem digest,
hypotheses, and global axioms.  It avoids retaining every root's prepared
source, cells, aggregate result, and intermediate source theorems at once,
which is the safer execution shape for the eventual 1,061-root policy.

## September 25 performance-note assessment

The source review in
`docs/advice/Candle_NL_Performance_Research_2026-09-25.md` helped separate
plausible costs, but subsequent authentic measurements give them sharply
different priorities.

Already covered or materially repaired:

- one reusable analytic soundness theorem covers the supported expression
  language, with one shared source program and shared jets;
- aggregate internal batches return one compact verdict rather than exposing
  full Taylor stacks or constructing one computed theorem per cell;
- ordinary exact arithmetic now prepares untrusted square-root hints, while
  reflected guards and the general soundness theorem remain decisive;
- compiled point plans and prepared cell variants are reused where their
  authenticated source envelope permits; and
- stable analytic support is checkpointed, while the new sequential adapter
  bounds retained proof state across roots.

Confirmed material costs:

- general rational arithmetic and normalization: the proved fixed-scale
  hybrid reduced a genuine 64-box numerical phase from 161.3204 to 63.0574
  seconds (**2.56x**) and the complete proof path by **2.15x**;
- remaining rational algebraic work: authentic rational additions and
  multiplications accounted for about **84.1%** of algebraic-plus-nonlinear
  force time and cost about **3.50x** as much per instruction as fixed
  polynomial steps;
- reusable preparation and theorem handoff: on the stratified 16-root sample,
  source/plan/cell preparation was comparable to or larger than numerical
  proof production; and
- enclosure quality: only 9 of the first 32 original leaves accepted whole,
  although all 32 closed by depth two in 60 final cells.  Subdivision count
  must therefore accompany every throughput comparison.

Measured lower-priority or negative candidates:

- numeric rather than 128-pair GCD fuel changed a 512-call control only from
  about 6.9 ms to 5.5 ms;
- constant and variable completion cost about 6 ms per genuine
  six-dimensional result;
- explicit interval endpoint-product sharing regressed, and a proved
  Hessian-only projection gave no stable batch benefit; and
- fixed evaluator setup is tens of milliseconds and is already amortized by
  aggregate calls.

The next useful discriminator is consequently not another isolated
micro-optimization.  It is a varied real-root theorem-producing batch with a
fixed adaptive policy, charging shared preparation once and measuring source
and plan preparation, selected-cell construction, compact `Kernel.compute`,
theorem extraction/root handoff, accepted domains, and final-cell inflation.
Only if the numerical phase remains dominant should residual rational
`inv`/`sqrt`/`atn` work or a wider bounded-scale representation move ahead of
preparation and handoff work.
