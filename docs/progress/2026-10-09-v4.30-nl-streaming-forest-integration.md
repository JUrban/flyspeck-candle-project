## Streaming question

The earlier genuine-forest discriminator evaluated all 11,718 authenticated
nodes, selected each lane's visited set offline, and then timed a filtered
batch.  That established the numerical and pruning opportunity, but did not
charge the control flow expected in the final checker.

The new development driver instead:

1. reads the authenticated 20-root, 11,718-node topology;
2. starts at those roots and parses numerical jobs only when they are reached;
3. evaluates one node;
4. prunes a strictly negative result, subdivides a recognized domain failure
   or nonnegative internal node, and rejects any nonnegative terminal leaf;
5. validates each reached child pair as an exact partition of its parent on
   the topology's prescribed axis; and
6. continues depth first until every root is discharged.

The prototype retains the full serialized job table as a development oracle
for the authenticated boxes.  It does not yet replace that table with a
compact root-plus-split certificate.  However, it reads the table as raw
data and converts only reached jobs, so numerical preparation and memory now
follow the actual streaming traversal.

Nine warmed, alternating repetitions charge serialized input reading,
topology validation, reusable program preparation, visited-job conversion,
online traversal, and numerical evaluation:

| Lane | Input | Reusable preparation | Traversal + visited preparation | Complete run | Peak RSS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Source DAG | 0.022797 s | 0.001228 s | 3.958779 s | **3.982845 s** | 69.9 MiB |
| Historical dihedral | 0.019290 s | 0.001175 s | 0.576486 s | **0.596987 s** | 17.9 MiB |

The historical lane is **6.867 times faster** in traversal and visited-node
preparation and **6.672 times faster end to end**.  The previously measured
filtered batches were 3.976066 seconds and 0.577105 seconds, a 6.890-times
ratio.  Online orchestration therefore preserves the material gain; charging
the full input lowers the ratio by only about 3.2%.

The historical streaming traversal itself differs from its earlier
preparation-plus-filtered evaluation by less than one millisecond in these
means.  Its approximately 20 ms total increase comes from reading and
validating the complete serialized certificate input.  A compact
root-plus-split representation may remove most of that recurring input cost,
but the cost is already small and is not the next performance target.

The two reusable native builds took 15.78 and 15.55 seconds.  The two complete
all-node oracle scans took 9.93 seconds and are validation-only setup, not part
of normal streaming checking.  These one-time costs are recorded separately.

## Decision and next proof boundary

The generally authenticated historical-dihedral instruction passes the native
architecture gate:

- it preserves complete certificate acceptance;
- it materially improves the complete online traversal, not merely one
  arithmetic kernel;
- the benefit comes chiefly from tighter bounds and fewer visited nodes;
- traversal overhead does not consume the improvement; and
- memory falls by approximately four times in this fixture.

This justifies continuing its bounded reusable soundness proof.  The next
proof packet is now:

1. consume the proved positive-reciprocal containment result in the
   historical root/denominator invariant;
2. prove the single historical instruction satisfies the existing universal
   interval/jet contract, including delta, triangle-`U`, square-root,
   arctangent, gradient, Hessian, and domain obligations;
3. connect the already authenticated three source-DAG motifs and their
   permutations to that instruction case; and
4. run the instruction through the verified streaming forest and measure
   computation plus compact theorem handoff.

The proof remains one general instruction theorem, not a per-inequality
calculus proof.  No padded-hardware proof is started.  A second genuine
expression family is still required before extrapolating the 6.67-times
complete-run result across the nonlinear workload.

The broader performance program also remains active.  Once the historical
instruction runs through `Kernel.compute`, its native/Candle ratio will test
the separate approximately 15-times execution-boundary problem.  The much
larger historical generic/specialized gap still requires complete numerical
algorithms and enclosure-driven search counts; this result closes one
important part of that gap but does not redefine it away.
