## Supported numerical-replay envelope

For a bounded scale calculation, apply only observed endpoints:

```text
supported cells = 23,465,427 archive terminals
                  * source-to-cell factor in [1.00000, 1.28302]

compute CPU = supported cells * compute rate in [0.52922, 0.58886] s/cell
post-ready CPU = supported cells * post-ready rate in [0.54800, 0.80852] s/cell
```

This gives:

| Boundary | Low observed-endpoint orientation | High observed-endpoint orientation |
|---|---:|---:|
| `Kernel.compute` only | **3,450 CPU-h** | **4,925 CPU-h** |
| complete post-ready stock route | **3,572 CPU-h** | **6,762 CPU-h** |

Using the four-sample weighted cell factor 1.03122 instead gives 3,557--3,958
CPU-hours for computation and 3,683--5,435 CPU-hours for the post-ready route.
Those narrower figures are not preferred forecasts; they merely show the
effect of the observed weighted inflation.

The envelope excludes:

- all 835 currently unsupported records;
- missing archive identities and any changed/failed certificate proposals;
- cold source preparation, certificate search, and checkpoint construction;
- corpus-wide proof-state growth or garbage-collection effects;
- a measured distribution for repair depth;
- inter-worker contention and failure/retry cost.

It may also overcharge work that a future batch shares across records.  The
correct conclusion is scale, not precision: the existing stock evaluator is
still in the several-thousand-CPU-hour regime.

## Memory and plausible wall-clock scale

Both controlled stock runs peak at 17.223 GiB RSS, and the TSKAJXY worker was
observed at about 17.2 GiB.  Ignoring shared-kernel/cache effects:

| Concurrent stock workers | Approximate worker RSS | Idealized wall time for 3,572--6,762 CPU-h |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 6 | 103 GiB | 24.8--47.0 days |
| 8 | 138 GiB | 18.6--35.2 days |

Eight workers fit only under the owner's exceptional 150 GiB allowance and
leave little room for checkpoints, the controlling process, and host load.
Ten such workers would require roughly 172 GiB before overhead.  The table
assumes perfect scaling and omits setup/retries, so it is a lower-bound-style
operational orientation, not a scheduling promise.

## Relationship to the fast route

On case 16594, the current G6 executable is reproducibly about 3.6x faster by
recurring CPU and uses 5.861 GiB rather than 17.223 GiB.  That is a useful
measured advantage, but it is one specialized case and it returns a callback
verdict rather than the original HOL theorem.  Applying 3.6x mechanically to
the stock envelope would still leave roughly 1,000--1,900 CPU-hours and would
ignore unsupported functions and the unfinished trusted verdict-to-theorem
connection.  It is therefore not reported as an all-NL fast forecast.

The route decision supported by current evidence is:

1. keep the stock reflected route as the trusted reference/fallback and as a
   way to validate additional strata;
2. make the first genuine fast original theorem the immediate production
   integration milestone;
3. measure fast and stock on the same complete checker contract after that
   theorem exists;
4. pursue specialized numerical instructions/algorithms where matched native
   experiments show large recurring wins, because evaluator dispatch alone
   cannot supply the desired orders-of-magnitude improvement.
