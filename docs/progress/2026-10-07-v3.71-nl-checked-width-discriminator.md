## Exact-width factorial

The preceding complete-family audit found that case16594 stores at most
64-bit rounded endpoints but reaches 135 bits in a quotient numerator.  This
packet holds the full 167-instruction derivative/Taylor graph, dyadic
`2^40` grid, exact outward shift rounding, boxes, and acceptance fixed while
changing Boost's checked fixed-size integer from 256 to 192 magnitude bits.

`fdb0646d4fd85e17bd72c8f205e624303c70f7973e4f84ea8686c82531080c9f`.
Both lanes execute 20,620,250 directed shift quotients and accept 875/875.
The narrower type is 0.996-times the speed of the 256-bit lane—slightly

Reducing the fixed backend from four 64-bit limbs to three therefore does not
explain the 6.25-times checked/hardware gap.  A further 160-bit alias would
use the same three-limb storage class and is not an informative next test.

## Mixed-width probe failed closed

A subsequent local smoke probe used checked 128-bit persistent arithmetic and
a checked 192-bit final Taylor accumulator.  It was intended to retain narrow
endpoints while widening only scale-squared recomposition.  Job 0 first
failed on the known `2 * scale * linear` multiplication.  After that term was
moved to the wide accumulator, the same job failed on another checked scalar
addition before producing a result.

This is useful negative evidence.  The v3.70 profiler measured interval
endpoint products, interval additions, quotient operands, and converted
inputs, but not every scalar accumulation in the Taylor machinery.  Its
observed maxima are insufficient to authorize a mixed-width lowering.  The
experimental wide-completion patch was removed rather than extended through
another isolated exception; the accepted 192/256 source and evidence remain
unchanged.

## Consequence for the larger performance programme

The broader investigation remains active, with two distinct measured
boundaries:

1. **Algorithm/enclosure architecture.**  The historical approximately
   340-times case10173 figure describes the initial generic-native route.
   Faithful specialized native work has already reduced that particular
   residual to about 1.35 times.  Its next work is transfer of the winning
   complete algorithm to the reflected checker, not another native
   expression micro-specialization.
2. **Verified execution and arithmetic.**  Matched computations remain about
   12--34 times slower inside Candle than in native exact execution.  On the
   independent case16594 graph, checked fixed-size native arithmetic remains
   6.25 times slower than the padded hardware discriminator.  Neither
   prepared dispatch nor three-versus-four-limb storage explains it.

The next bounded arithmetic packet must begin with one mechanical operation
ledger for the complete lowered checker: every stored value, scalar
accumulation, product, quotient numerator, and narrowing point, with its
required width measured on several real families.  From that ledger, one
native prototype may use narrow checked endpoints plus explicitly typed wide
temporaries and a total overflow result.  It must preserve exact output or
conservative enclosure, accept complete certificate batches, and fall back
to the existing exact path on overflow.  A substantial multi-family batch
win—not a single-expression microbenchmark—is the gate for any CakeML
bounded-word compute extension.

In parallel, the proof-safe path continues to flatten the natural-number
`Kernel.compute` batch and reduce the 12--34-times evaluator boundary.  The
existing exact-angle specialization stays bounded: completing a small
reusable proof is reasonable, but this negative result does not justify new
case-specific derivative layers.
