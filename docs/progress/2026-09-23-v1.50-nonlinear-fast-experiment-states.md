## Minimum-state split for nonlinear experiments

The experimental lanes now use the smallest faithful prerequisite state:

| Experiment | Minimum state | Full nonlinear verifier required? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Change exact expression, box, or encoded certificate data; compare numerical representations; time `Kernel.compute`; exercise accepted and rejected checker inputs | Reflected polynomial/interval checker checkpoint | No |
| Change the checker, reifier, compiler, or derivative definitions | Clean predecessor plus a rebuild of the reflected-checker checkpoint | No |
| Prove the language-wide `diff2c`, gradient, Hessian, and Taylor correspondence | Taylor-library plus reflected derivative checkpoint | No |
| Capture real Flyspeck leaves or certificate inputs; prove the mechanical connection to an original Flyspeck formula; compare the complete old and reflected leaf paths | Full authenticated nonlinear predecessor | Yes |

Thus only the last integration boundary remains coupled to the complete
certificate/fixture load.  A definition-changing failure invalidates its
small state and starts again from the clean predecessor; a data-only driver
always starts from a fresh copy of the stable reflected-checker checkpoint.
No experiment continues from a partially loaded or failed state.

## Universal whole-box soundness result

Candle commit `49131f5cd9eb0bd3a809c69ac088dcc780a05f26` establishes the
dimension-generic correspondence between the reflected expression language
and Flyspeck's derivative/Taylor vocabulary.  Commit
`f62ab72f8122a82ebded8794ef8f95bb01d2d98f` completes the proof-producing
whole-box checker boundary.

The final theorem `candle_poly_dim_whole_box_accept_sound` states that, for any
dimension, supported valid expression, and valid exact-rational box list of
that dimension, acceptance by the computed whole-box checker implies that the
original real-valued polynomial is strictly negative at every point of the
corresponding Flyspeck Cartesian interval.  The proof supplies the following
reusable contract once for the whole expression language:

- exact list/vector lower, upper, center, and radius correspondence;
- computed center enclosure soundness;
- reflected gradient values equal Flyspeck `partial` values;
- reflected Hessian values equal Flyspeck `partial2` values;
- list-fold accumulators equal the finite sums in Flyspeck's Taylor theorem;
- the general `m_cell_domain`, `diff2_domain`, and `m_taylor_error` premises;
- exact-rational acceptance implies a negative real Taylor upper bound.

Consequently, a new expression in the supported language does not require a
new hand-written calculus or Taylor proof.  It requires data/reification and a
mechanical connection to the original Flyspeck formula, followed by an
instantiation of the language-wide theorem.
