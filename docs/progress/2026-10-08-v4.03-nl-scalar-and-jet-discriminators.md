### Reciprocal computation is not the native bottleneck

The fixed nonlinear path performs 50,750 reciprocal interval operations over
the batch.  An untrusted preparation pass captured their exact input/output
stream.  The supplied lane checked the position, exact input, sign, interval
shape, and two exact endpoint-product inequalities before consuming each
output.  Reordered records, an invalid interval, and an overflowing endpoint
product all failed closed.

Nine alternating measured repetitions produced:

| Lane | Preparation | Evaluation | Fully charged batch | Accepted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Compute reciprocal by division | 0.062393 s | 0.576759 s | 0.639153 s | 875/875 |
| Read and validate supplied reciprocal | 0.134248 s | 0.575116 s | 0.709364 s | 875/875 |

The result bounds are byte-identical.  Replacing division with validation
improves recurring evaluation only **1.0029 times** and makes the fully charged
batch about **1.1099 times slower** because the 3,096,559-byte certificate
stream must be read and parsed.  The one-time capture itself takes 0.0620
seconds of preparation plus 0.6485 seconds for evaluation and writing.

This closes the native performance question for this candidate.  The general
checked reciprocal predicate remains useful in Candle because it avoids
implementing a word long-divider, but no larger reciprocal-specialization
proof project is justified by speed.  It is bounded at the already general,
source-independent interval rule.

### General fixed arctangent range reduction is material

The existing fixed arctangent implementation was unnecessarily restricted to
arguments in `(-1,1)`, although its scalar library already contained the
standard reciprocal range reduction.  The candidate now uses that range
reduction, still fails closed at exact `+/-1` until a pi/4 boundary is
supplied, and leaves generic jet propagation unchanged.

Nine alternating measured repetitions produced:

| Lane | Preparation | Evaluation | Fully charged batch | Accepted |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Rational arctangent and derivatives | 0.058223 s | 0.577867 s | 0.636090 s | 875/875 |
| Fixed outward range-reduced arctangent | 0.060791 s | 0.431034 s | 0.491825 s | 875/875 |

This is a **1.3407-times recurring** and **1.2933-times fully charged**
improvement.  A separate complete 875-box run checked every fixed-kernel
Taylor result against the rational reference enclosure.  All 875 candidate
bounds are slightly wider, by at most about `1.46e-11`, while the smallest
margin below zero remains about `0.00109036`; acceptance remains 875/875.

This candidate has a bounded proof case: one reusable theorem for the
source-independent arctangent instruction, its reciprocal identities, and
its outward alternating-polynomial bounds.  It does not require per-expression
calculus.  The proof should not grow into a case16594-specific specialization.

## What the same-batch profile says

The rational-arctangent lane's 167 source instructions account for 0.5691
seconds of its 0.5779-second evaluation.  Grouping source instructions by
operation gives:

| Operation group | Profiled time | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Arctangent | 0.165338 s | 29.05% |
| Polynomial evaluation | 0.126570 s | 22.24% |
| Generic multiplication | 0.102541 s | 18.02% |
| Inverse | 0.052789 s | 9.28% |
| Pi/2 construction | 0.046053 s | 8.09% |
| Square root | 0.040337 s | 7.09% |
| Addition and negation | 0.035455 s | 6.23% |

The fixed arctangent result is therefore consistent with the profile rather
than a surprising microbenchmark.  Conversely, the reciprocal experiment
shows that the inverse group's cost is mostly its generic derivative/Hessian
and Taylor propagation, not endpoint division.

The earlier published 2-by-2 endpoint/graph factorial remains complementary:
it measured 1.58--1.81 times from graph work and 1.52--1.73 times from endpoint
representation, with all four lanes accepting 875/875.  Thus both generic jet
work and arithmetic representation matter, but neither scalar division nor
another small local rewrite can plausibly supply orders of magnitude by
itself.

## Keeping both large gaps explicit

The original approximately 15-times Candle/native observation and the
historical approximately 340-times generic-native/specialized-C++ observation
diagnose different boundaries.  The latter was measured on case10173 and is
not a literal residual ratio for case16594; on the already optimized direct
case10173 path, native fixed-scale is about 0.0610 seconds versus roughly
0.0823 seconds for the historical specialized object.  It nevertheless
remains an important architectural warning: good enclosure quality does not
imply comparable work per box, and a generic dense jet evaluator can perform
far more arithmetic than a specialized numerical verifier.

For case16594, the new fixed-arctangent candidate lowers the relevant native
evaluation reference from about 0.58 seconds to about 0.42 seconds.  This
makes the need to eliminate Candle execution, allocation, conversion, and
proof-handoff overhead more—not less—visible.  The earlier approximately
225.8-second reflected control is not yet directly comparable to the new
kernel because the complete checked-word Candle executor has not run.

## Concrete route to the larger gain

The next decisions remain gated by a complete authenticated batch:

1. Finish the translated compact checker using the completed 128/192-bit
   Taylor state, checked square-root and reciprocal certificates, and a
   general range-reduced fixed-arctangent instruction.  Execute the actual
   167-instruction program over all 875 boxes and require 875/875 acceptance.
2. Charge authenticated plan preparation, input conversion, recurring
   numerical execution, fallback work, compact result production, theorem
   handoff, full elapsed time, and peak memory separately.  Retain the
   existing below-22.58-second 10-times gate and below-2.258-second 100-times
   architectural gate against the reflected control.
3. If encoded traversal or allocation dominates, execute the identical word
   operations through an authenticated fixed-layout plan: fixed tuples or
   arrays, sparse six-gradient/21-Hessian state, and coarse straight-line
   blocks.  Prove one plan-compiler simulation theorem rather than calculus
   theorems per expression.  Hold boxes, rounding, final bounds, and
   acceptance fixed.
4. If general jet propagation dominates, add a small vocabulary of generally
   proved fused jet instructions selected by authenticated source-to-plan
   compilation.  Delta/radicand and coordinate-square-root instructions are
   the first evidence-backed candidates; arbitrary case-specific source
   rewrites are not.  Compare full batch work and enclosure tightness after
   each addition.
5. If theorem handoff dominates, return one compact batch verdict and use a
   reusable soundness theorem, rather than reconstructing intermediate
   arithmetic theorems per leaf.
6. Validate any material architecture on case10173 or a second real
   expression family before enlarging its proof surface.  Complete-batch
   elapsed time and certificate acceptance remain the deciding measures.

The fixed-arctangent proof is therefore worth a bounded reusable effort; the
reciprocal performance branch is closed; and the main investment remains the
complete checked-word executor plus a general authenticated plan/compiler
boundary capable of removing repeated generic jet work.
