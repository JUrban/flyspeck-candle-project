## Outcome

The compiled historical checker no longer treats supplied square-root and
reciprocal intervals as authenticated only by executable Boolean tests.  Its
HOL4 proof branch now exposes the exact endpoint inequalities implied by every
successful certificate.

For a square-root certificate, the theory proves:

- validity of both input and output intervals;
- nonnegativity of all four endpoints; and
- `output_lower^2 <= 2^40 * input_lower` together with
  `2^40 * input_upper <= output_upper^2` in signed integer endpoint
  semantics.

For a reciprocal certificate, the theory proves strict sign preservation and
the appropriate `2^80` cross-product bounds separately for positive and
negative sign-stable intervals.  These are the standard outward reciprocal
conditions at the checker's fixed `2^40` scale.  The proof also connects the
two-word exact product helper to ordinary natural multiplication and proves
that the stored two-word scale-squared constant is exactly `2^80`.

The contracts are general over all accepted certificate bytes.  No captured
root or reciprocal value is trusted, and no fixture-specific equality appears
in the theorem statements.
