## Result

The reusable LP architecture now makes individual real terminals faster than
the colocated legacy checker after preparation. A deduplicated master table
contains exact source-row encodings and hypothesis-free denotation theorems.
Untrusted terminal plans name master entries by integer index and provide their
own terminal theorem and multiplier. Candle accepts a plan entry only if the
stored source conclusion exactly matches the supplied theorem conclusion.

For each master entry Candle now proves one theorem generalized over the
certificate weight. Terminals specialize that theorem rather than rebuilding
the sparse-row denotation. The terminal's own inequality theorem is still used
to construct its source assumptions, so no assumptions from unrelated master
rows are imported.

On unchanged real `hard_2.dat` terminals 8 and 10 with a 502-variable union
basis and 1,017 unique master rows:

| Phase | Terminal 8 | Terminal 10 |
|---|---:|---:|
| colocated legacy proof | 20.93 s | 21.54 s |
| indexed row denotation | 1.12 s | 0.76 s |
| source row-holds conversion | 1.27 s | 1.37 s |
| `ALL` list construction | 5.13 s | 5.38 s |
| `ALL_MAP` and residual source-proof work | 0.31 s | 0.46 s |
| complete sparse `Kernel.compute` verdict | 12.34 s | 11.25 s |
| final generic soundness handoff | <0.01 s | <0.01 s |
| **complete computed terminal** | **20.22 s** | **19.23 s** |

Terminal 8 is 3.4% faster than legacy; terminal 10 is 10.8% faster. Both final
theorems match legacy in conclusion, exact hypothesis set, `equals_thm`,
printed theorem digest, and axiom set. These are the first measured real LP
terminal wins for the complete computed checker, including source-proof and
soundness work after reusable preparation.

## Cold preparation remains open

The same batch deliberately charges all reusable work once:

| Cold or batch phase | Wall seconds |
|---|---:|
| computed source-plan reconstruction for both terminals | 16.31 |
| one shared selector/context preparation | 16.20 |
| verify 1,017 master rows, including weight-general theorems | 84.57 |
| construct both integer-index terminal plans | 0.20 |
| both computed terminal proofs | 39.50 |
| **complete computed batch** | **156.78** |
| complete legacy batch | 52.08 |

Thus the cold two-terminal batch remains 3.01x slower. This is not an overall
verification speedup yet. It establishes that the per-terminal architecture is
competitive and shifts the main problem to preparation reuse and reduction.
The weight-general master step itself adds approximately 10.4 seconds to
one-time preparation relative to the prior master, while saving approximately
15 seconds across these two terminals; it amortizes within this pair.

## Fine-grained recurring costs

The passing v4 profile showed that master-index resolution costs less than one
millisecond. Before weight generalization, reconstructing weighted row
denotations cost 8.28--8.89 seconds per terminal. Source-proof preparation
split into approximately 0.71 seconds for row-holds conversion, 4.62--5.23
seconds for linear `ALL` construction, and 0.25--0.41 seconds for `ALL_MAP`.

Weight-general master theorems reduce row denotation to 0.76--1.12 seconds.
The remaining recurring target is therefore `ALL` construction, followed by
the 11--12 second sparse numerical fold. A faster source-proof assembly must
preserve the exact terminal assumptions.

## Important negative result

A seemingly cleaner alternative replaced per-row rewrite conversions with
pre-proved generic sparse-row, row-holds, and `ALL`-cons rules. It preserved
both exact terminal theorems but was much slower in Candle. Row instantiation
rose to 11.60/14.08 seconds, source-proof preparation rose to 35.79/32.58
seconds, and the two computed terminals took 117.88 seconds instead of 54.97.
That commit was reverted completely. The evidence rules out repeated generic
`MATCH_MP`/type-instantiation over the growing `ALL` tail as the next approach.

## External preparation boundary

The experiment supports an external plan format containing:

- a canonical union basis;
- a deduplicated master source-row table with sparse integer encodings; and
- per-terminal master indices and multipliers.

Such data remains untrusted. Inside Candle, exact source conclusions authorize
master entries, hypothesis-free kernel theorems establish their denotation,
and every terminal index is checked against its supplied source theorem before
use. A future serialized plan may avoid repeated host parsing and search, but
hashes or indices alone must never authorize theorem content.

The next useful experiments are:

1. reduce or batch the linear `ALL` proof assembly without the failed generic
   `MATCH_MP` pattern;
2. measure one larger real family with a single union master to determine true
   cold amortization across many terminals;
3. investigate whether an externally generated master encoding can be checked
   by a single reusable table-correspondence theorem rather than 1,017 separate
   host-driven constructions; and
4. retain the existing complete sparse verdict and soundness theorem while
   profiling any numerical-representation changes separately.
