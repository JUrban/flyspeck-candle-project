## Performance-note assessment

The 25 September research note identifies useful source-level candidates, but
the current evidence assigns them different priorities.

Already measured or substantially covered:

- The fixed-scale checker took **13.101306 s** on 64 genuine boxes, versus
  **105.142893 s** for the rational checker, an **8.03x** difference with
  **130/130** structurally equal results.  Rational normalization and GCD work
  are therefore the primary measured explanation of the current gap.
- Shared jets and internal batches already avoid the old 43-program expansion
  and amortize evaluator setup and theorem-boundary costs.
- Source preparation and box construction are already reported separately as
  reusable cold costs rather than being charged per checked box.

Still credible but not yet measured end to end:

- constant, zero, and sparse nodes may perform avoidable dense completion;
- full-jet operations may compute value and gradient data when only a Hessian
  is retained;
- repeated GCD-fuel construction matters on rational fallback paths;
- untrusted point-hint preparation should use ordinary arithmetic rather than
  manufacture proof objects that the trusted checker discards; and
- compact verdicts may reduce result conversion and theorem-handoff cost.

The next discriminating experiment remains a warm batch of varied genuine
boxes, with expression preparation charged once.  It should separately time
setup/input conversion, fixed numerical execution, compact verdict conversion,
and final theorem handoff, while recording acceptance/subdivision behavior,
integer growth, and memory.  A constant/zero or Hessian-only variant is worth
adding to that batch if it preserves the checked algorithm.  Running this after
strict acceptance and the complete genuine-leaf theorem will avoid profiling
another incomplete proxy.
