## Exact executable multiplication boundary

The bounded-word branch now contains a general full `word64` product rather
than only a benchmark-specific calculation.  It splits both inputs into
22/22/20-bit chunks, performs the nine exact chunk products as small natural
numbers, propagates radix-`2^22` carries, and assembles the low and high
`word64` results.

HOL4 proves for arbitrary inputs that the returned pair reconstructs the
mathematical product exactly:

```text
w2n low + 2^64 * w2n high = w2n left * w2n right
```

The proof includes general extraction, radix reconstruction, convolution,
carry, and two-word assembly lemmas.  It is not a finite test theorem.  A
separate closed program repeats the translated primitive 10,000,001 times and
checks its exact digest before exiting.  CakeML's verified translator accepts
the implementation and the verified x64 compiler accepts the closed program.
Focused final builds reported 34 seconds for the logical theory, 47 seconds
for the base translations, 90 seconds for the closed program translation,
and 115 seconds for verified x64 compilation.  These are reusable preparation
costs, not recurring product costs.

## Keeping both larger gaps explicit

This packet attacks only the verified-execution side.  The broader numerical
architecture investigation remains active:

- the historical approximately **340-times** generic-native versus
  specialized-C++ result was a starting case10173 comparison; accumulated
  controlled native specialization has reduced that particular fixture's
  residual to about 1.35 times;
- the independent case16594 factorial assigns only about 1.045 times to safe
  generic derivative/Taylor lowering and about 3.22 times to exact versus
  padded-hardware arithmetic, while the current reflected/native boundary is
  about **98 times**; and
- the successful full-word microbenchmark does not establish that the
  128/192 mixed trace, complete certificate, or theorem-producing executor
  will preserve its projected gain.

The next cheap native algorithm experiment is therefore orthogonal to the
word work: one source-authenticated, enclosure-tight instruction for a real
85-step case16594 inverse/square-root/arctangent block.  It will run under
both existing exact-mixed and padded-hardware backends.  This holds arithmetic
and rounding fixed while changing the analytic data flow, and then holds the
data flow fixed while changing arithmetic.  It must retain 875/875 acceptance
and report preparation, recurring batch time, memory, and bound deltas.
Earlier dispatch preparation, check removal, generic sharing, and simply
deferring the 85-step completion were negative or lost acceptance, so they
will not be repeated.

The proof effort stays bounded at the now-complete general full-word product.
No proof of a case-specific 85-step instruction begins unless the native
complete-batch experiment shows a material recurring win and useful
enclosure quality.  A winning instruction should be expressed as a reusable
source operation with a general containment theorem, not as a theorem for one
named inequality.
