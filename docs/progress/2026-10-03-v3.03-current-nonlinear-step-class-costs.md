## Question and method

After correcting the fixed-outer scheduler interpretation and ruling out
transition conversion as a material cost, the useful question is where the
current proved checker spends its numerical time.

A new bounded probe used the same 128 authenticated case-10173 cells. It
retained the complete current checker but, depending on mode, repeated one
selected authentic program step before discarding that copy and executing the
unchanged step used for the accepted verdict:

- fixed class: polynomial pushes and fixed outer algebra;
- nonlinear class: square root, inverse, arctangent, and pi-half; and
- matched controls: identical instruction classification without the repeated
  step.

The source program has 44 fixed-class and 10 nonlinear-class top-level steps.
Every call accepted all 128 cells, returned a closed result, and left the axiom
inventory unchanged. Two observations per class were run in symmetric order.

## Result

| Mode | Run 1 | Run 2 | Mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Fixed-class control | 24.683 s | 21.255 s | **22.969 s** |
| Fixed-class duplicate | 34.251 s | 31.830 s | **33.040 s** |
| Nonlinear-class control | 21.959 s | 20.447 s | **21.203 s** |
| Nonlinear-class duplicate | 31.224 s | 31.832 s | **31.528 s** |

The within-class duplicate deltas are:

- fixed polynomial and outer algebra: **10.072 seconds**;
- nonlinear operations: **10.325 seconds**.

The two increments sum to 20.397 seconds, close to the ordinary 21--23-second
128-cell checker scale. They split 49.38% fixed versus 50.62% nonlinear. This
is not an exact additive profiler: repeated work can change allocation and GC,
and the controls themselves differ by about 1.77 seconds. The near-equal,
independently repeated increments are nevertheless strong evidence that both
classes are material under the current full fixed-outer scheduler.

Normalized only as a descriptive workload statistic, one duplicated nonlinear
step costs about 8.07 ms per cell versus 1.79 ms for one fixed-class step, or
about **4.51x** as much. There are 4.4 times as many fixed-class steps, which is
why the two total class costs are nearly equal.

Peak sampled RSS was unchanged at 17,982,344 KiB.

## Plan consequence

The older fixed/rational-hybrid profile correctly identified expensive dense
rational outer algebra for that implementation. The proved full fixed-outer
checker removed much of that cost. On the current checker, nonlinear work is
no longer secondary: ten nonlinear instructions consume roughly the same
aggregate time as forty-four fixed polynomial/algebraic instructions.

A useful optimization program now has two independent measured lanes:

1. improve fixed Taylor operations or representation without widening bounds;
2. isolate the nonlinear class into square-root versus inverse/arctangent
   work, again behind one internal evaluator boundary.

The nonlinear split is the cheaper next discriminator because it involves
only ten instructions and may identify one reusable primitive target. No new
soundness proof should begin until that bounded real-data measurement finds a
material winning boundary.
