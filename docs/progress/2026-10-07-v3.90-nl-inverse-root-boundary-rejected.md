## Corrected discriminator

The v3.89 proposed direct quotient experiment would have duplicated too much
of the already rejected v3.84/v3.85 whole-chain implementation.  The narrower
question is where that implementation first loses enclosure quality.

The new native screen recognizes the same three exact source chains through
the authenticated identities

```text
numerator = -d(delta)/dx[4..6]
radicand  = 4*x[1..3]*delta.
```

It changes only the three source subsequences

```text
radicand; sqrt; inverse
```

into a direct jet for `1/sqrt(radicand)`, using the general first- and
second-derivative identities and one Taylor completion.  It then returns to
the ordinary accepted sequential evaluator for multiplication by the
numerator, arctangent, addition of `pi/2`, and the remainder of the source
program.

The control and candidate both use:

- the source-authenticated numerator and radicand instructions;
- sign-classified interval products;
- checked mixed 128/192-bit exact arithmetic;
- the same `2^40` grid and directed outward rounding;
- the same supplied square-root certificates and 875 genuine boxes; and
- the unchanged final acceptance test.

This therefore holds arithmetic, rounding, later Taylor composition, and
certificate data fixed while changing only the inverse-root derivative/data
flow.

## Performance decision

No soundness proof will be written for this direct inverse-root instruction.
Even if its enclosure could be repaired without losing the measured saving,
its observed complete-batch ceiling is only about eight percent on this
family.  The current specialization proof remains bounded to the two positive
coordinate-parametric delta families:

- `4*x_i*delta`; and
- `-d(delta)/dx_j`.

Those instructions retain 875/875 acceptance and together improve exact
native recurring evaluation by 1.839 times.  Their general source-recognition,
identity, and universal-jet containment lemmas remain worthwhile reusable
work; the inverse-root and whole-chain variants do not.

This result supersedes the v3.89 next-native-discriminator wording.  Another
case16594 chain fusion is not justified unless it has a mathematically tighter
inverse-root boundary and a native prefix first shows materially more than the
present eight-percent ceiling.  Cheap component diagnostics may be used to
understand a future new method, but not to accumulate local shims.

## Larger-gap plan

The original roughly 340-times generic-native/specialized-C++ comparison and
the formal-execution gap remain different questions.  On case10173, controlled
native algorithm work has already reduced the former residual to about 1.35
times.  On case16594, the positive delta instructions and sign classification
produce a 1.138-second exact accepted computation, while the current reflected
batch takes about 225.8 seconds.

The next high-value packet is therefore unchanged by this rejection:

1. build the actual 875-cell source-authenticated bounded-word executor now
   that the 6.34-second compiled operation trace passed its gate;
2. charge decode/conversion, real data flow, fallback, result production,
   compact theorem handoff, memory, and repeated complete-batch time;
3. require byte-identical exact bounds and 875/875 acceptance; and
4. test the positive analytic instructions and executor architecture on a
   second genuine expression/certificate family before broad proof or
   production integration.

For the numerical-algorithm lane, the next substantial native candidate must
come from a distinct low-work enclosure algorithm or second family, not from
deleting another completion in these three chains.  The same two-axis
factorial remains mandatory: compare generic versus specialized data flow
under fixed exact arithmetic, then compare exact versus hardware arithmetic
with the winning graph held fixed.  Preparation, complete-batch time, bound
quality, memory, and full certificate acceptance decide the gate.
