## Discriminator result

The bounded follow-up proposed in v2.96 completed on the first 128 exact
genuine cells of the case-16594 certificate. A later source audit corrected
the original description of the two implementations. It compared:

1. the established fixed-outer checker, which retains fixed-scale negation,
   addition, multiplication, and square throughout the program; and
2. an experimental scheduler that falls back to rationals for all four
   operations while a later nonlinear instruction remains, then selects fixed
   scale for the final algebraic suffix.

Both closed computed verdicts accepted all 128 cells and had no assumptions.
The experimental scheduling policy was nevertheless materially slower:

| Phase | Wall seconds |
|---|---:|
| Encode the same 128 genuine jobs | 6.952099 |
| Established fixed-outer checker | 67.917256 |
| Experimental rational-before-nonlinear scheduler | 101.049514 |

The experimental computation took **1.488x** the established time, or
**48.78% longer**. Peak sampled RSS was unchanged at 17,982,344 KiB. The
established control was only 2.84% slower than the earlier 66.038987-second
128-cell measurement, so the negative result is much larger than the observed
control-run variation.

This is a numerical architecture discriminator, not a soundness result for
the experimental evaluator. No source theorem or universal invariant is
claimed for it. Its role was to determine whether the scheduling policy
deserved proof investment. It does not: rational fallback before the final
algebraic suffix should not be generalized or integrated.

## Interpretation

The successful acceptance comparison rules out an immediate coverage problem
on this prefix. The timing rejects the experimental rational fallback: giving
up the established fixed-outer representation before later nonlinear
instructions costs enough exact-rational work to be 48.78% slower here. This
result supports retaining the proved fixed-outer checker; it does **not** show
that fixed-scale multiplication or square is slower than the established
rational implementation.

This narrows the useful optimization search:

- retain the current established checker as the measured baseline;
- do not spend proof or integration effort on the rational-before-nonlinear
  scheduler;
- discriminate the remaining numerical costs at a finer boundary before
  changing representation again, particularly nonlinear primitives and
  fixed/rational conversion points; and
- continue the second-expression complete-certificate integration so that
  conclusions are not based on case 16594 alone.

The result also reinforces the staging rule from the architecture review:
numerical candidates must win on varied genuine data before receiving a
universal soundness proof and complete source-theorem integration.
