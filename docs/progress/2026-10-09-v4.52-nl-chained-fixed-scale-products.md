## Outcome

The HOL4 checked-word layer now proves sound composition across successive
fixed-point multiplications.  In particular, if the first operand encloses a
logical numerator at scale `2^40`, the second checked multiplication encloses
the product numerator at scale `2^80`.

This closes the scaling gap that prevented the existing one-step interval
multiplication theorem from being reused for chained expressions.  The
concrete `bounded_interval64_mul3` helper now has a corresponding theorem:
successful execution on three intervals enclosing integer values produces an
interval enclosing their three-way product at scale `2^80`.

The proof is general over accepted intervals and values.  It does not trust a
captured fixture or assume a sign class.  It proceeds by:

1. proving a general four-corner product enclosure for arbitrary ordered
   integer intervals;
2. instantiating the already-proved checked multiplier at all four endpoint
   corners;
3. lifting each endpoint fact by the positive fixed scale; and
4. selecting the applicable lower and upper corners through the general
   enclosure theorem.

Thus the proof does not duplicate the multiplier implementation's nine-way
sign dispatch.
