Date: 2026-10-09 UTC

## Outcome

The fast signed word-level product has been connected to mathematical integer
semantics in an isolated CakeML branch.  The new HOL4 results prove:

- the exact integer value returned by widened floor and ceiling multiplication;
- preservation of that value when the widened result is narrowed to one word;
- the floor and ceiling quotient inequalities at scale `2^40`;
- for all four sign combinations, a successful directed floor is below the
  exact scaled signed product and a successful directed ceiling is above it;
- executable sign tests agree with nonnegativity and nonpositivity of the
  represented integer;
- the two-corner interval helper returns endpoints bounding its selected
  corner products; and
- the mixed-sign helper's minimum and maximum enclose all four products it
  computes.

This discharges the arithmetic core needed by a full interval-multiplication
refinement theorem.  It does not yet prove that every value selected from two
input intervals is enclosed by `bounded_interval64_mul`; that final theorem
must combine endpoint validity, sign-class dispatch, multiplication
monotonicity, and the helper contracts above.

## Implementation notes

The proof deliberately follows the implementation boundary:

1. prove the widened signed result's exact integer value;
2. use the existing fail-closed narrowing theorem to transfer that value;
3. prove quotient/remainder rounding bounds; and
4. lift those bounds through the two interval-product constructors.

This avoided a brittle case split that unfolded widening, directed rounding,
and narrowing simultaneously.  A quotient/remainder proof also replaced a
generic arithmetic tactic that was slow on the `2^40` constant.  The resulting
`compute_boundedInterval` theory rebuild completes in about four seconds once
its ordinary prerequisites are present.
