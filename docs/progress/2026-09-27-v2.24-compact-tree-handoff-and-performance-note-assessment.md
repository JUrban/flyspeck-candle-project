## Assessment of the September 25 research note

The source review in
`docs/advice/Candle_NL_Performance_Research_2026-09-25.md` has been useful.
Subsequent focused measurements distinguish its hypotheses as follows.

### Already covered

- One reusable analytic theorem covers every supported expression; the
  genuine formula does not receive a hand-written calculus proof.
- Shared source programs, compiled point plans, multi-box internal batches,
  aggregate acceptance theorems, and compact computed verdicts are already in
  the proved path.  Large diagnostic Taylor stacks do not cross the production
  theorem boundary.
- Proof-producing `REAL_RAT_REDUCE_CONV` work was removed from untrusted
  square-root hint generation.  Ordinary exact `Num` arithmetic proposes the
  data and the reflected checker remains authoritative.
- Authenticated checkpoints preserve expensive stable libraries.  This run
  measures a further 103.94-second reduction in cold support loading, although
  it does not change recurring numerical work.

### Material measured explanations

- **Rational representation and normalization.**  The proved fixed-scale
  hybrid reduced a genuine 64-box compute from 161.3204 to 63.0574 seconds
  (**2.56x**) and its complete proof from 180.7978 to 83.9137 seconds
  (**2.15x**).
- **Residual rational algebra.**  On eight authentic boxes, the fallback
  partition used about 82.2% of measured top-level instruction time.  The
  refined partition found rational additions and multiplications about 3.50x
  as expensive per instruction as fixed-polynomial steps and responsible for
  about 84.1% of algebraic-plus-nonlinear force time.
- **Preparation and coverage.**  Only 9 of 32 original leaves accepted whole;
  the proved adaptive forest needed 60 final cells.  Reusable source/point
  preparation and variant construction remain material, so seconds per box
  cannot be interpreted without accepted-domain and subdivision counts.
- **Theorem handoff.**  The new experiment confirms it is material, but also
  shows that constructing endpoint-equality topology theorems individually is
  not the compact solution by itself.

### Lower priority after measurement

- Numeric rather than 128-pair GCD fuel changed a 512-call control only from
  0.0069 to 0.0055 seconds.  General rational normalization matters; the fuel
  representation does not explain the large gap.
- Constant and variable completion measured about 6 ms per six-dimensional
  result.
- Explicit sharing of interval endpoint products regressed substantially, and
  a proved Hessian-only projection gave no stable genuine-batch benefit.
- Fixed evaluator setup is tens of milliseconds and is already amortized by
  internal batching.  It does not explain minute-scale warm computations.
- A generic ordinary interpreter was slower than Candle's fast evaluator, so
  moving the unchanged algorithm outside `Kernel.compute` is not indicated.

## Consequence for staging

The note helps explain the remaining gap, but not through a single cost.  The
current priorities remain:

1. preserve the general analytic theorem and compact numerical verdict;
2. extend the proved bounded-scale representation across the measured hot
   rational algebraic path while checking bound tightness and subdivision;
3. keep source/certificate preparation reusable and measure varied real boxes
   with shared work charged once; and
4. if the tree boundary is pursued, test topology as compact checked data
   under a general soundness theorem rather than constructing all large
   endpoint equalities through per-node tactic work.

The last item is the narrow follow-up suggested by this experiment.  Until it
beats the established path on the same real batch, the exact-tree adapter
should not be cited as a runtime improvement.  The measured GCD-fuel,
constant, endpoint-sharing, and Hessian-only ideas should remain deferred
unless a new authentic profile contradicts the existing controls.
