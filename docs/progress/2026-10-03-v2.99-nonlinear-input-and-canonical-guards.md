## Findings

Two focused case-10173 experiments tested costs identified after the complete
one-verdict milestone. Neither candidate should be integrated as a production
optimization.

### Exact job-encoding breakdown

Every adaptive leaf under one root repeats the same seven whole-box
square-root intervals. A data-only encoder therefore constructed that payload
once for each of the 3,305 roots, reused it across 4,173 cells, and rebuilt the
remaining center/domain payloads separately. Its final complete cval job term
was alpha-equivalent to the exact input retained by the accepted case-10173
compute theorem. No checker, numerical data, or theorem changed.

| Preparation component | Wall seconds |
|---|---:|
| Encode 3,305 shared root payloads | 60.211 |
| Flatten 4,173 root/cell references | 0.000446 |
| Encode 4,173 center-certificate payloads | 67.410 |
| Construct and encode 4,173 leaf domains | 62.430 |
| Assemble cells and the final job list | 2.215 |
| Candidate preparation total | **192.267** |
| Exact identity comparison with retained input | 0.705 |

The established flat encoder measured 202.374 seconds in the complete run.
The separated/shared construction therefore saved 10.107 seconds, or 4.99%,
in this run. A coarser first version measured 197.905 seconds before identity
comparison, showing that allocation order and retained intermediate terms can
move this small difference. Root sharing alone is not a material architecture
gain.

The important result is the decomposition. Root certificates, center
certificates, and leaf domains each cost roughly one minute; list flattening
and assembly do not dominate. Even eliminating all 62.430 seconds of host
domain construction would save only 6.45% of the current 967.368-second
prepared-plan path, before charging any replacement computation inside the
evaluator. A topology-driven domain reconstruction design is therefore not
the next proof investment unless a bounded numerical prototype also reduces
the much larger `Kernel.compute` cost.

### Direct well-formedness versus canonical rebuild

Candle already contains a universally proved alternative to raw certificate
canonicalization. The established guard rebuilds the complete canonical cval
term and compares it with the input. The alternative scans the same input for
well-formedness directly and is proved extensionally equal to the established
checker.

The two complete-checker calls ran back-to-back on the same preserved first
128 case-10173 jobs and the same 250 authentic topology tokens:

| Canonical guard | `Kernel.compute` wall |
|---|---:|
| Established rebuild and compare | **24.593 s** |
| Direct well-formedness scan | **25.397 s** |

Both produced the identical closed numerical/topology result, with no
hypotheses and no axiom growth. The direct scan was **3.27% slower**. It does
not remove a real-data bottleneck and should not replace the established
guard.

This also resolves one open point from the performance research note: the
apparent duplicate-certificate allocation is not responsible for the current
case-10173 evaluator gap under the large-heap configuration.

## Plan consequence

The complete case-10173 recurring path remains approximately:

- 202 seconds exact input encoding;
- 688 seconds numerical/topology `Kernel.compute`; and
- 69 seconds compact theorem/source handoff.

The new breakdown rules out host list construction, simple root-payload
sharing, and direct canonical well-formedness as high-value targets. The next
bounded discriminator should measure the repeated per-job source-program
work: exact square-root-slot checking and certificate patching versus the
Taylor evaluator proper. A version that temporarily omits exact-slot walks on
already authenticated valid data can establish an upper bound before any
effort is spent proving a fused check-and-patch traversal. No such unchecked
variant may contribute theorem evidence.
